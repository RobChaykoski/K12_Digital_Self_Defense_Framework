# Digital Self-Defense: Foundations for an AI-Mediated World
## What Every 18-Year-Old Needs to Know — Taught From Kindergarten to Grade 12

**Version:** 1.0 — October 2026  
**Research Question:** *"What cybersecurity and AI self-defense competencies should every student possess by the time they graduate from secondary school, regardless of whether they pursue a career in computing?"*

---

## 🎯 The Core Research Question

Every 18-year-old — the future artist, the tradesperson, the nurse, the teacher, the entrepreneur, the parent — needs to defend **five things** in an AI-mediated world:

| # | The 18-Year-Old Needs to Defend | Why It Matters |
|---|----------------------------------|----------------|
| 1 | **Identity** | Who they are online can be stolen, cloned, or forged |
| 2 | **Data** | What they generate (clicks, location, preferences) is sold and used to predict them |
| 3 | **Finances** | Scams, subscriptions, and crypto can drain a bank account in minutes |
| 4 | **Reputation** | A screenshot at 14 can shape a university application at 18 |
| 5 | **Decision-Making** | AI feeds, chatbots, and agents shape what they think, buy, and believe |

This document maps each of those five needs to **developmental progression** from Kindergarten through Grade 12, showing:
- **What the student learns** at each age band
- **How the existing personas** carry each concept forward
- **Why it's not overbearing** — each grade band adds only one new layer

---

## 🧭 The Spiral Curriculum Model

This framework uses a **spiral curriculum** (Bruner, 1960): concepts are revisited at every grade band with increasing depth, always anchored by the same characters. A student who met **Locky the Fox** in Kindergarten meets **Locky's red-ribbon key** on Cipher's hoodie in Grade 6, and sees **Cipher hand it to the Grade 12 analyst class** in Grade 12.

The result: **familiarity without repetition.** The student feels they *already know* the material because the same faces and voices appear — but the content is always new.

## 🔗 Five Competency Arcs — K-12 Progression

### ARC 1: Identity — "Who am I, and who says they are?"

| Grade Band | The Question | Persona | Session | Not Overbearing Because |
|------------|--------------|---------|---------|------------------------|
| **K-2** | "Do I know this person?" | Locky (the Fox) | S2: The Password Lock | Concrete: a lock and a key. No jargon. |
| **3-5** | "Is this message really who it claims to be?" | Detective Dot | S1: Real vs. Fake Email | Detective game format, not a test |
| **6-8** | "Is this account, voice, or video real?" | Cipher + Echo + Lens | S2: Lock It Down / S3: The Clone Game / S4: Spot the Fake | Hand-on: they *enable* MFA, they *run* a clone game |
| **9-10** | "What evidence proves identity?" | Cipher + Lens | S3: The Clone Wars / S4: Deepfake Lab | Forensic checklist — they *judge*, not just receive |
| **11-12** | "What system proves identity?" | Cipher + Signal | S3: Clone Wars Deep Dive / S11: Media & Algorithm Analysis | Provenance, metadata, digital signatures |

**Not overbearing:** Identity starts as "do I know you?" and ends as "can the system prove you?" — but the same lock-and-key visual anchors every level.

### ARC 5: Decision-Making — "What should I do?"

| Grade Band | The Question | Persona | Session | Not Overbearing Because |
|------------|--------------|---------|---------|------------------------|
| **K-2** | "Red = stop, yellow = think, green = go" | Captain Shield | Every session | Traffic light game — physical, not mental |
| **3-5** | "Pause. Read. Think." | Captain Shield + Dot | Every session | Detective pause — hover the link before clicking |
| **6-8** | "PAUSE → VERIFY → ACT" | Cipher | S1, S8, S9, S10 | Incident commander playbook — structured decision tree |
| **9-10** | "What's my risk, and is it worth taking?" | Cipher + Signal | S8 + S10 | Threat model their school account |

**Not overbearing:** Decision-making starts as "traffic light" and ends as "AI agent permission framework." The same Pause Button (Captain Shield → Cipher) is the through-line.

## 🪜 Why This Isn't Overbearing

The framework avoids overwhelming students through **four design principles:**

### 1. One Concept Per Session
Each session teaches exactly **one core skill**. A Grade 4 student doesn't learn phishing, AI, and data in the same 40-minute block. They learn *one* — today it's phishing. Next week it's AI. The concepts spiral back; they don't stack.

### 2. Same Characters, New Depth
A student who meets **Pixel the AI Scanner** in Grade 4 meets **Echo (voice forensics)** and **Lens (visual forensics)** in Grade 7 — characters who are clearly Pixel's successors. The student thinks *"Oh, I know this team!"* before the content hits them.

### 3. Physical → Visual → Digital Progression
- **K-2:** Painted footprints on paper, physical padlocks, red/yellow/green cards
- **3-5:** Magnifying glass app, match cards, passphrase word chain
- **6-8:** MFA setup on tablet, voice recording game, screenshot preservation
- **9-12:** Reverse image search, metadata analysis, token simulation, case-file binding

The medium changes, but the *mental model* stays the same.

### 4. The Stop & Think Anchor
Every session begins or ends with **Captain Shield's Pause Button**. K-2 students press a paper shield. Grade 6 students say *"PAUSE."* Grade 12 students write *"Decision logged: verified → executed."* Same instinct, different vocabulary. The anchor is always present — students never wonder *"where am I?"*

## 🧠 Decision Architecture: How Each Grade Thinks

| Grade Band | Decision Style | Example |
|------------|---------------|---------|
| **K-2** | Physical response (stop / go / tell) | "Free Robux!" → freeze statue → show adult |
| **3-5** | Pattern recognition (fishy / not fishy) | Email sender + urgency words + link → spot 2 of 3 clues |
| **6-8** | Protocol execution (PAUSE → VERIFY → ACT) | Discord message → check sender → click link in new tab → verify |
| **9-10** | Risk assessment (is this worth it?) | Crypto token → check contract → compare to rug-pull patterns → decide |
| **11-12** | System design (how do I prove this?) | Deepfake audio → check wave form + metadata + reverse search → file in case |

**The cognitive load grows naturally.** No grade band asks students to think at a level they haven't developed.

## 🔑 The 5-Point Check: Does This Lesson Fit?

Every session in the framework should pass this check. If it doesn't, it's either:
- **Too technical** (should be simplified)
- **Too advanced** (should move to a higher grade band)
- **Duplicative** (should be merged)

### The Five Questions
1. **Which of the 5 arcs** (Identity, Data, Finances, Reputation, Decision-Making) does this session teach?
2. **Which persona** owns this concept?
3. **What age band** is this for? Is this developmentally appropriate?
4. **What is the one skill** the student can do after this session?
5. **How does this connect** to what they learned in the previous phase?
