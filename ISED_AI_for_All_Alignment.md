# ISED "AI for All" — Framework Alignment

**Strategy:** Canada's National Artificial Intelligence Strategy: *AI for All* (Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada)
**Source:** ised-isde.canada.ca — "Canada's National Artificial Intelligence Strategy: AI for All"
**Framework version aligned:** 3.0
**Last verified against source:** October 2026

---

## 1. What "AI for All" actually is

*AI for All* is Canada's **national AI strategy**, published by ISED. It is not an education programme and it is not a curriculum. It is an industrial and policy strategy organised around three values — **trust, opportunity, and sovereignty** — with **adoption** as the mechanism connecting them.

This distinction matters for how a K-12 framework should cite it. Only two of its six pillars bear directly on school curriculum. The other four are about industrial capacity, compute, company scaling, and international trade, and claiming curriculum alignment with them would be theatre.

## 2. The six pillars

| # | Pillar | What it covers | Relevance to K-12 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Protecting Canadians and safeguarding democracy** | Privacy and online safety law, AI safety capability, deepfakes and synthetic media, election integrity, watermarking and transparency, a Canadian AI Safety Institute, a Trusted AI Certification programme | **Direct.** Deepfakes, online harms, and children's privacy are named explicitly |
| **2** | **Empowering Canadians** | AI literacy for all, workforce training, K-12 and post-secondary education, Indigenous leadership in AI, official-language and accessibility commitments | **Direct.** This is the pillar a school framework belongs under |
| **3** | **Powering AI adoption for shared prosperity** | SME adoption, public-service delivery, industrial AI, priority sectors | Indirect — career context only |
| **4** | **Building the Canadian sovereign AI foundation** | Sovereign compute, data, talent, infrastructure | Not applicable to K-12 |
| **5** | **Scaling Canadian champions** | Growth capital, government as anchor customer | Not applicable to K-12 |
| **6** | **Building trusted partnerships and global alliances** | Standards alignment, international co-investment, market access | Not applicable to K-12 |

## 3. What the strategy commits to that a school can actually use

These are the specific, quotable commitments relevant to this framework. They are worth citing because they are concrete and current:

- **National AI Literacy Initiative** — free, accessible AI training for all Canadians.
- **K-12 teacher training** — a commitment to *double* AI training to more than **3,000 educators**, with AI learning kits for classrooms.
- **CanCode** — **$30 million** to fund not-for-profit organisations delivering free digital-skills training, *including coding, AI, and emerging technologies, to youth from kindergarten to Grade 12 and their educators*, with emphasis on underrepresented groups.
- **AI transparency and watermarking** of AI-generated content, so Canadians can tell when they are interacting with AI.
- **Canadian AI Safety Institute** — $50 million to track emerging AI risks and evaluate models.
- **Canada Trusted AI Certification** — a programme to help people identify trustworthy AI products.
- **Modernised privacy law, online safety law, and legal tools to combat deepfakes** — the strategy names deepfakes used as a form of sexual violence, particularly against women and children.

That last point is the strongest single justification for this framework's sextortion and deepfake units: the federal strategy itself frames non-consensual synthetic intimate imagery as a harm to be legislated against, not merely a social-media etiquette problem.

## 4. How this framework maps to the strategy

### Primary alignment: Pillar 2 (Empowering Canadians)

| Strategy commitment | Where this framework delivers it |
|---|---|
| Foundational AI literacy: understand, use, and build with AI | Phase 2 introduces real vs. synthetic media; Phase 3 covers voice cloning and manipulation; Phase 4 covers deepfake forensics and provenance |
| Identifying bias, misinformation, privacy risks, and unsafe uses | Phase 4: algorithm audit, provenance briefs, confidence statements; Phase 2–3: misinformation and manipulation |
| Teacher-facing AI learning kits | `docs/teacher_onboarding.md`, session plans, and assessment materials are written to be teachable without specialist AI background |
| Reaching learners regardless of region | Paper-based delivery paths for Programs 1–2; Program 3 has a no-device alternative |

### Secondary alignment: Pillar 1 (Protecting Canadians)

| Strategy commitment | Where this framework delivers it |
|---|---|
| Legal tools to combat deepfakes | Program 3 legal panel — Criminal Code s.162.1, extortion s.346, and the limits of what a victim can do alone |
| Safeguarding children's information and online activity | Password and identity hygiene (Phase 3–4), privacy settings audits (all phases), data minimisation in every activity |
| AI transparency and watermarking | Provenance-first media analysis; students learn to ask *where did this come from* rather than *does it look fake* |
| Online safety and reporting | The 4-Ds sextortion protocol, Cybertip.ca routing, evidence preservation |

### Explicitly not claimed

This framework does **not** align with Pillars 3–6, is **not** an ISED resource, and receives **no** ISED funding or endorsement. It is an independent curriculum that draws on the literacy and safety commitments of the national strategy. Saying so plainly is more useful to a board than an inflated crosswalk.

## 5. Classroom activities drawn from the strategy

These replace the earlier list, which referenced legislation and programmes that do not exist or are no longer current.

- **Pillar debate (Phase 4):** students research one of the *six* pillars and argue for where its next dollar should go. Requires them to distinguish an industrial strategy from a school policy — a genuine media-literacy exercise.
- **Literacy gap analysis:** the strategy reports Canada ranked 44th of 47 countries on AI training and literacy. Students examine what that means locally: who in their community has had AI training, and who has not.
- **Transparency audit:** find three AI-generated items in the wild. Which are labelled or watermarked? Which are not? What would a Trusted AI Certification need to show?
- **AI careers:** research roles in AI safety, evaluation, policy, and accessibility — the parts of the ecosystem the strategy funds, not just model-building.
- **Community AI project:** design a tool for an underserved group, then identify which pillar it would sit under and why.
- **Deepfake law brief:** read the strategy's language on deepfakes as sexual violence, then compare it to Criminal Code s.162.1 and to what the major platforms' own policies actually enforce.

## 6. Corrections in this version

Version 1.0 of this document contained several claims that were not accurate against the published strategy:

| Claim in v1.0 | Status |
|---|---|
| The strategy has **seven** pillars, including a "Pan-Canadian AI Research Strategy" pillar and an "AI for All Accessibility" pillar | **Wrong.** Six pillars, named differently. See §2 |
| **AIDA (AI & Data Act)** is a current legislative framework | **Stale.** AIDA did not proceed. The strategy instead commits to modernising privacy law and introducing online safety law |
| **"Canada.AI"** and **"G7 Canada.AI"** are federal AI programmes | **Not real.** No such programmes |
| **"CPTPP AI guidelines for Indigenous data sovereignty"** | **Not real.** The strategy's Indigenous commitments run through the National AI Literacy Initiative, Canadian Heritage's Indigenous Languages Program, NRC's Indigenous Languages Technology Program, and First Languages AI Reality at Mila |
| **"Canada's 5-pillar AI strategy"** | **Stale.** A previous framing; superseded |
| **"$125M"** attributed to the strategy | **Unsupported** as stated |
| "AI for All Accessibility" as a pillar | **Not a pillar.** Accessibility is a commitment within Pillar 2 |

Any document elsewhere in this repository that still cites the seven-pillar structure, AIDA, "Canada.AI", or the CPTPP claim should be treated as superseded by this file.
