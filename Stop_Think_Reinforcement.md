# 🛑 Stop & Think — The Core Cyber Safety Principle
## Reinforcing Pause, Breathe, and Ask for Help Across All Phases

**Version:** 1.0 — Updated October 2026  
**Cross-Reference:** Integrates with ISED AI for All, ECNO Digital Wellness, and all program phases

## 🧠 Why "Stop & Think" Matters

The foundation of digital self-defense is not a specific skill — it's the **habit of pausing** before acting online. Every threat (phishing, deepfakes, scams, social pressure, anxiety) exploits speed. The antidote is intentional slowness.

### The Three Pillars:
1. **STOP** — Pause before clicking, sending, sharing, or spending
2. **THINK** — Ask: "Does this make sense? Who is this from? What happens if I act?"
3. **ASK / BREAK** — If unsure, ask a trusted person. If overwhelmed, take a break and come back

This principle is **developmentally adaptive**:
- **K-2:** "Stop, look, listen" (physical habit)
- **Grades 3-5:** "Stop, read, think" (cognitive habit)
- **Grades 6-8:** "Stop, analyze, decide" (critical thinking)
- **Grades 9-12:** "Stop, evaluate, act strategically" (strategic habit)

## 📚 Stop & Think by Phase

### Phase 1 (K-2): "The Little Traffic Light"
> *Red means stop, yellow means think, green means go.*

| Concept | Age-Appropriate Language | Example |
|---------|------------------------|---------|
| **STOP** | "When you see something tricky, freeze like a statue!" | Pause before tapping a game button |
| **THINK** | "Ask yourself: Does this look like a button you know?" | "Is this the green Play button or the red Free Robux button?" |
| **ASK** | "When in doubt, ask your grown-up" | Show a teacher or parent |
| **BREAK** | "Take a deep breath. You can try again later" | Close the tablet, do something else, come back |

**When to Stop & Think:**
- Before clicking any link or button
- Before sharing a photo or video
- When a message says "Act NOW!"
- When a screen makes you feel worried or confused
- When someone asks for a password

**Teacher Script:**
> "Remember our traffic light? Red = stop your fingers. Yellow = use your thinking brain. Green = now you can tap! When you see something that makes you go 'hmm,' hit the yellow button — that means take a breath and ask someone."

---

### Phase 2 (Grades 3-5): "The Digital Detective's Pause"
> *Great detectives don't rush — they observe, analyze, and verify.*

| Concept | Age-Appropriate Language | Example |
|---------|------------------------|---------|
| **STOP** | "Before you tap, press pause" | Hover over a link, read the sender address |
| **THINK** | "Ask: Is this real or fake? Is this too good to be true?" | "Free Robux for clicking? Hmm, let me check." |
| **ASK** | "When the clues don't match, ask a friend or grown-up" | Share a suspicious message with parents |
| **BREAK** | "If your brain feels buzzy, close the tab. Come back fresh" | Step away for 5 minutes, then re-examine |

**When to Stop & Think:**
- Before clicking any link (email, text, game chat)
- Before downloading anything
- Before entering a password
- When a social media post makes them feel inadequate
- When an AI image or video looks "off"
- When a notification causes a quick emotional reaction (excitement, fear, FOMO)

**Teacher Script:**
> "Digital Detectives know that the best ones don't rush. When you see something that makes your stomach flip or your eyes widen — that's your body's STOP signal. Press pause. Use your detective magnifying glass (check the sender, check the link). If the clues don't add up, ask a fellow detective (a friend or grown-up). And if your brain feels like a fuzzy bee — take a break. Fresh eyes solve the hardest cases."

---

### Phase 3 (Grades 6-8): "Own Your Space: Analyze Before You Act"
> *Your digital footprint is permanent. Your decisions are powerful.*

| Concept | Age-Appropriate Language | Example |
|---------|------------------------|---------|
| **STOP** | "Hover before you hop. Screenshot before you swipe" | Hover over suspicious Discord link |
| **THINK** | "What's the story here? Who benefits? What's missing?" | "This AI voice says Mom needs $50 — but she's at work" |
| **ASK** | "If you're stuck, loop in a trusted adult" | Share sextortion screenshot with guidance |
| **BREAK** | "If the screen is too much, step away. Your worth isn't tied to a notification" | Step outside for fresh air after a stressful chat |

**When to Stop & Think:**
- Before responding to a DM from a stranger
- Before sending a photo or video
- Before accepting a friend request
- Before commenting on a heated post
- When a game voice chat sounds "robotic"
- Before entering personal info on a new website
- When multiple notifications fire at once (FOMO storm)

**Teacher Script:**
> "Own Your Space means you decide what comes in and what goes out. The first move in owning your space is the STOP. When a notification buzzes, when a message pops up, when a video starts playing — that's your moment. Hover. Screenshot. Read carefully. Does the story make sense? Who benefits if you act fast? If you're not sure, ASK — loop in a teacher, parent, or counsellor. And if the screen starts feeling heavy — Step Away. Take a breath. Walk outside. Your worth doesn't depend on responding in 30 seconds."

---

### Phase 4 (Grades 9-12): "The Wild West: Think Before You Invest"
> *The internet rewards the informed and punishes the impulsive.*

| Concept | Age-Appropriate Language | Example |
|---------|------------------------|---------|
| **STOP** | "Never let urgency decide for you" | Pause before wiring crypto, clicking a link, accepting a deal |
| **THINK** | "What's the evidence? What's the counter-argument? What happens 24 hours from now?" | "This AI deepfake says school is cancelled — check the official account" |
| **ASK** | "When in doubt, get a second opinion" | Show a suspicious link to a tech-savvy friend or parent |
| **BREAK** | "If you're making decisions tired, hungry, or emotional — sleep on it" | Don't buy crypto at 2 AM. Don't send that text at midnight. Come back fresh. |

**When to Stop & Think:**
- Before entering credentials on any site
- Before connecting a crypto wallet
- Before sharing a photo/video publicly
- Before accepting a friend/follow request from a stranger
- When an AI tool asks for permissions
- Before responding to a deepfake audio call
- When social media triggers comparison or anxiety
- Before making any financial decision online
- When multiple platforms are firing notifications simultaneously

**Teacher Script:**
> "In the Wild West, the ones who survive aren't the fastest — they're the most careful. Here's the law: **Never let urgency decide for you.** When a message says 'Act NOW!' or a chart says 'Limited Time!' — that's your STOP signal. Think: What's the evidence? What's the counter-argument? What happens tomorrow if I act today? Ask: Get a second opinion. Text a friend. Check an official source. Break: If you're scrolling at midnight and feel the spiral starting — close the app. Sleep on it. The best digital defense is a well-rested brain."

## 🧰 Stop & Think Tools & Resources

### For Students — "The 4-Second Rule"
Before any action online, count to 4. In those 4 seconds, ask:
1. **Who** is this from? (Verify sender/contact)
2. **What** do they want? (Link, info, money, photo)
3. **Why** now? (Urgency, FOMO, surprise)
4. **What if** I'm wrong? (Reversible or irreversible?)

### For Teachers — "The Pause Button Protocol"
- Build a physical "PAUSE" button (cardboard or plastic) for classroom use
- When a student says "I'm confused about this screen," they can press the pause button
- The class pauses together for 10 seconds of collective thinking
- Normalizes stopping mid-lesson when a concept feels too fast

### For Parents — "The Home Break Rule"
- Designate tech-free zones/times (dinner, bedroom after 9 PM)
- Model the behavior: "Mum/Dad is taking a screen break"
- If a child is upset by something online, the rule is: **Stop the screen, name the feeling, take 5 deep breaths, then talk about it**

## 📝 Stop & Think Checklists by Context

### Phishing Context
- [ ] **STOP** — Before clicking any link
- [ ] **THINK** — Does the sender address match the claimed company?
- [ ] **THINK** — Is there urgency? ("Act NOW!", "24 hours left")
- [ ] **THINK** — Is the link preview different from the text?
- [ ] **ASK** — Show a parent/teacher if unsure
- [ ] **BREAK** — Step away for 2 minutes if the email caused anxiety

### Deepfake / AI Context
- [ ] **STOP** — Before sharing or reacting to unusual media
- [ ] **THINK** — Are there unnatural details? (Hands, lighting, lip-sync)
- [ ] **THINK** — Does the story match known facts?
- [ ] **ASK** — Check official sources before accepting
- [ ] **BREAK** — If confused, come back with fresh eyes

### Sextortion Context
- [ ] **STOP** — Before paying or responding
- [ ] **THINK** — Can I afford it? Will they ask again?
- [ ] **THINK** — Did I send the original photo publicly or privately?
- [ ] **ASK** — Screenshot before blocking/reporting
- [ ] **BREAK** — Remember: one person's opinion ≠ your worth

### Crypto / Money Context
- [ ] **STOP** — Before sending money or connecting a wallet
- [ ] **THINK** — Is this a guarantee or a guess?
- [ ] **THINK** — Is the urgency real or manufactured?
- [ ] **ASK** — Get a second opinion from someone financially savvy
- [ ] **BREAK** — Sleep on it. Real opportunities don't expire at midnight

## 📋 Implementation: How to Weave Stop & Think Into Every Session

### Every Session Should End With:
1. **A "Stop & Think" checkpoint** — students practice the principle with a real example
2. **A "When to Ask for Help" reminder** — naming specific people/resources
3. **A "Break" prompt** — encouraging rest between screens

### Assessment Integration:
- **Knowledge:** "Name 3 situations where you should STOP before clicking"
- **Skill:** Given a suspicious message, demonstrate the Stop → Think → Ask/Break sequence
- **Attitude:** Students reflect on when they *didn't* stop and what happened

### Parent Communication:
- Include "Stop & Think" tips in every parent handout
- Suggest family "screen break" times
- Encourage modeling: parents narrate their own Stop & Think moments ("Mum is seeing a weird ad — pause, let me think about it")
