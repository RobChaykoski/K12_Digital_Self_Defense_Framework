# K-12 Digital Self-Defense & Cyber Safety Education Framework
## "The Wild West Online: Navigating New & Old Threats"

**Prepared for:** Local School Board / Community Education Initiative  
**Based on:** Digital Self-Defense & Cyber Safety Month Campaign (October 2026)  
**Target Audience:** Kindergarten through Grade 12 (K-12)  

---

## 🗺️ Framework Overview

This progressive education framework takes students from foundational digital literacy in Kindergarten through advanced threat detection in Grade 12. It covers both **legacy threats** (phishing, social media traps) and **emerging threats** (AI/Deepfakes, Sextortion, Crypto Scams) in age-appropriate ways.

The "Wild West" metaphor helps students understand that the internet is a place of opportunity but also requires protection, awareness, and smart decision-making — much like frontier towns needed both cowboys and sheriffs.

---

## 📚 PHASE 1: Elementary (K-5)
### Theme: "My Digital Body & Online Space"

#### Kindergarten - Grade 2 (Ages 5-8)
**Goal:** Build foundational awareness of online safety through storytelling and routine habits.

| Topic | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Who am I talking to?** | Identify familiar vs. unfamiliar people online | "Stranger Danger" digital version with cartoon characters |
| **The Password Lock** | Understand passwords as keys to your door | Create a physical "password lock" craft; practice saying passwords out loud (not sharing) |
| **Clicking Safely** | Distinguish safe links vs. suspicious links | Game: "Tap the Green Button, Not the Red One" |
| **Screen Time Boundaries** | Recognize healthy tech habits | Family screen-time contract; visual timer activities |
| **Digital Footprint** | Understand that online actions stay forever | "Footprints in the Sand" analogy — what disappears vs. what stays |

**Key Takeaway:** *The internet is like a playground — you can have fun, but always know who's watching and where your gate is.*

#### Grades 3-5 (Ages 8-11)
**Goal:** Introduce basic phishing, social media basics, and early AI awareness.

| Topic | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Phishing Basics** | Spot a fake email or message | "Real vs. Fake" worksheet with school newsletter examples |
| **Social Media Traps** | Understand likes, comments, and comparison | "The Highlight Reel" — social media shows only the best moments |
| **Password Power** | Create stronger passwords | Passphrase building exercise (e.g., "BlueTiger$RunsFast!") |
| **Intro to AI** | What is a computer making things? | Compare AI-generated art vs. human-made art; discuss prompts |
| **The "Too Good to Be True" Rule** | Identify basic scams | Story-based learning: free Robux, free Fortnite skins, etc. |

**Key Takeaway:** *Not everything online is real — and that's okay if you know how to check.*

## 📚 PHASE 2: Middle School (Grades 6-8)
### Theme: "Own Your Space: Privacy, AI & Digital Self-Defense"

#### Grades 6-8 (Ages 11-14)
**Goal:** Hands-on skills for account security, social engineering, and digital coercion.

| Pillar | Focus Area | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Pillar 1: Account Fortification & Scams** | Phishing via Discord/Roblox/Snapchat; account takeovers via fake login links | Understand credential stuffing and password reuse | Create a password manager; practice identifying fake login pages |
| **Pillar 2: AI & Synthetic Media** | Voice cloning for prank calls or "freemium" game currency scams; spotting synthetic imagery | Learn the "Family Safe Word" to counter voice clones | Record family safe words; play AI voice clone audio samples |
| **Pillar 3: Sextortion & Digital Coercion** | Stranger danger in gaming chats; moving chats to unmonitored secondary apps (Snap/Insta) | Master "Stop, Screenshot, Block, Report" protocol | Role-play sextortion scenarios with discreet reporting |

**Key Protocols Taught:**
- 🔒 **The 3 Do Nots of Sextortion:**
  1. Do NOT pay or negotiate (paying always leads to more demands)
  2. Do NOT delete evidence immediately (screenshot first!)
  3. Do NOT panic alone (loop in a trusted adult)

**Silent & Discreet Resources:**
- Take It Down (takeitdown.ncmec.org) — Anonymous tool to stop explicit images
- NeedHelpNow.ca — Confidential guidance on image sharing and cyberbullying
- Kids Help Phone / Cybertip.ca — 24/7 crisis support lines

**Key Takeaway:** *Your digital self is yours to protect. Know your boundaries, lock your accounts, and never be afraid to ask for help.*

## 📚 PHASE 3: High School (Grades 9-12)
### Theme: "The Wild West: Con Artists, Crypto & AI"

#### Grades 9-12 (Ages 14-18)
**Goal:** Advanced threat detection, financial literacy online, and real-world application.

| Pillar | Focus Area | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Pillar 1: Account Fortification & Scams** | Credential stuffing; password reuse across university/job portals; fake brand/collab offers | Understand multi-factor authentication (MFA) and password managers | Set up app-based 2FA; audit personal email for reused passwords |
| **Pillar 2: AI & Synthetic Media** | Hyper-realistic voice clones targeting parents (fake emergency/bail calls); visual deepfake manipulation | Learn basic image forensics (EXIF data, lighting inconsistencies) | Deepfake detection workshop using free tools |
| **Pillar 3: Sextortion & Digital Coercion** | Coercive control; financial sextortion rings (Snapchat/Instagram DMs); non-consensual image sharing | Understand the business model behind sextortion syndicates | Analyze real case studies from Cybertip.ca |
| **Pillar 4: Crypto Scams** | NFT drops, "rug pulls," airdrop scams, fake exchange promotions | Distinguish legitimate crypto projects from pump-and-dump schemes | Simulated crypto investment exercise with loss/gain tracking |
| **Pillar 5: Social Media Traps (Advanced)** | Algorithmic echo chambers, engagement bait, influencer affiliate traps | Understand how platforms monetize attention | Audit personal social media feed for ad patterns |

**Key Protocols Taught:**
- 🔍 **AI Detection Checklist:** Check for unnatural hand proportions, inconsistent lighting, mismatched shadows, audio lip-sync errors
- 💰 **Crypto Scam Red Flags:** "Guaranteed returns," pressure to act fast, unknown wallets, too-good-to-be-true airdrops
- 📱 **Social Media Hygiene:** Privacy settings audit, tag review, location tagging habits, follower growth vs. engagement rate analysis

**Key Takeaway:** *The internet rewards the informed and punishes the impulsive. Think before you click, invest, or share.*

## 🎯 Cross-Curricular Integration

This framework can be integrated into existing subjects:

| Subject | Integration Point |
|---------|------------------|
| **Computer Science** | Password hashing, encryption basics, how AI generates content |
| **Mathematics** | Probability of password guessing, crypto math (blockchain basics), data analysis of social media trends |
| **English/Language Arts** | Writing phishing emails, analyzing persuasive online content, digital storytelling |
| **Social Studies** | Digital citizenship, online privacy laws, global cybercrime networks |
| **Health/Physical Education** | Screen time impact, eye strain, posture, mental health from social comparison |
| **Art** | AI-generated art vs. traditional art; deepfake visual analysis |

## 📅 Suggested Delivery Schedule

### Option A: Monthly "Cyber Safety Moments" (Recommended)
- **15-minute assemblies or classroom sessions** once per month
- Rotating themes throughout the school year
- Aligned with Cybersecurity Awareness Month (October) for full campaign push

### Option B: Dedicated "Digital Literacy" Period
- Integrated into weekly schedule (30-45 min/week)
- More comprehensive coverage but requires curriculum allocation

### Option C: Parent-Student Joint Workshops
- Quarterly sessions where families learn together
- Builds home-school consistency for younger students

## 📊 Assessment & Progression

### Knowledge Checks by Level:

| Grade Band | Assessment Method |
|-----------|------------------|
| **K-2** | Visual recognition quizzes (point to the safe link); oral questioning |
| **3-5** | Worksheet completion; group role-play scenarios |
| **6-8** | Practical lab (set up 2FA, identify fake login page); scenario-based multiple choice |
| **9-12** | Project-based assessment (audit a social media account for privacy settings); written reflection on a real-world scam case |

### Progression Milestones:

- ✅ **Elementary:** Can identify safe vs. unsafe links; knows password basics; understands digital footprint concept
- ✅ **Middle School:** Can set up 2FA; identifies phishing emails; understands sextortion protocols; recognizes basic AI-generated content
- ✅ **High School:** Can audit accounts for security; detects deepfakes; evaluates crypto scams; explains algorithmic bias on social media

## 💡 Why This Framework Works

1. **Progressive Complexity:** Concepts build year-over-year, reinforcing and expanding prior knowledge
2. **Age-Appropriate Language:** No jargon without explanation; metaphors replace technical terms where needed
3. **Action-Oriented:** Every lesson ends with a practical skill students can use immediately
4. **Discreet & Judgment-Free:** Especially for sextortion, protocols avoid shame and embarrassment
5. **Future-Proof:** Designed to adapt as new threats emerge (add pillars without replacing existing ones)
6. **Family-Centric:** Extends learning home through parent guides and shared activities
