## 🗺️ Framework Overview

This progressive education framework takes students from foundational digital literacy in Kindergarten through advanced AI literacy and threat detection in Grade 12. It integrates government-backed AI education resources with practical digital self-defense skills, covering both **legacy threats** (phishing, social media traps) and **emerging AI-driven threats** (deepfakes, voice cloning, synthetic media, crypto scams) in age-appropriate ways.

The "Wild West" metaphor helps students understand that the internet is a place of opportunity but also requires protection, awareness, and smart decision-making — much like frontier towns needed both cowboys and sheriffs.

### 🔄 AI Education Integration

The framework incorporates three AI literacy resources, added in v2.0 and carried forward:

1. **ISED AI Literacy Initiative** — foundational knowledge about how AI models learn, work, and integrate into daily tools; data privacy fundamentals; ethical guardrails
2. **AI Literacy For All Curriculum** — pedagogical agency for educators; Indigenous data sovereignty; cross-curricular AI applications; self-paced educator modules
3. **ISED Canada's National AI Strategy — AI for All** — a six-pillar national strategy covering safety, adoption, sovereign capability, and international partnership. The framework aligns with what the strategy actually commits to; see [ISED_AI_for_All_Alignment.md](ISED_AI_for_All_Alignment.md) for the crosswalk and, importantly, for the pillars this framework does **not** claim to serve.

**Key design choices:**
- Progressive AI curriculum from "What is AI?" (K-2) through "Audio Forensics & Legal AI" (Grades 9-12)
- Indigenous data sovereignty woven through all grade bands
- Ethical AI guardrails and data privacy as cross-cutting themes
- Educator development track via AI Literacy For All self-paced modules
- **Provenance before perception:** students are taught to verify *where* media came from before judging whether it *looks* real. This is a deliberate change in v3.0 — see [Synthetic Media and Misinformation](#synthetic-media-and-misinformation).

## 🏛️ Framework Architecture: Four Stages

The framework is delivered as four developmental stages. Each has one exit competency, stated in the first person so a student can answer the question themselves.

| Stage | Grades | Goal | Exit competency |
|-------|--------|------|-----------------|
| **1. Digital Safety Foundations** | K-2 | Build foundational awareness of online safety | *I know when to stop and ask for help.* |
| **2. Digital Self-Defense Foundations** | 3-5 | Introduce basic cybersecurity concepts and risk recognition | *I can recognise common risks and protect my information.* |
| **3. Digital Resilience** | 6-8 | Develop skills in authentication, verification, and incident response | *I can recognise manipulation, verify requests, protect accounts, and respond safely.* |
| **4. Cybersecurity & Digital Independence** | 9-12 | Develop advanced cybersecurity thinking and independent decision-making | *I can assess digital risk, select appropriate protections, verify information, respond to incidents, and make responsible independent decisions.* |

The five competency threads that run through all four stages — Identity, Data, Finances, Reputation, and Decision-Making — are documented in [Digital_Self_Defense_Foundation.md](Digital_Self_Defense_Foundation.md).

## 📚 Phase 1: Elementary (K-5)
### Theme: "My Digital Body & Online Space"

### Kindergarten - Grade 2 (Ages 5-8)
**Goal:** Build foundational awareness of online safety through storytelling and routine habits.

| Topic | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Who am I talking to?** | Identify familiar vs. unfamiliar people online | "Stranger Danger" digital version with cartoon characters |
| **The Password Lock** | Understand passwords as keys to your door | Create a physical "password lock" craft; practise saying a *made-up* password out loud so the idea of a secret is concrete — never a real one |
| **Clicking Safely** | Distinguish safe links vs. suspicious links | Game: "Tap the Green Button, Not the Red One" |
| **Screen Time Boundaries** | Recognise healthy tech habits | Family screen-time contract; visual timer activities |
| **Digital Footprint** | Understand that online actions stay forever | "Footprints in the Sand" analogy — what disappears vs. what stays |

**AI Literacy Integration:**
- **What is a Computer Making?**: Introduce AI as a "helper that learns from examples" using picture books and comparison activities
- **Indigenous Storytelling Connection**: Use traditional storytelling patterns to explain how AI "learns" from stories and patterns it has seen

**Key Takeaway:** *The internet is like a playground — you can have fun, but always know who's watching and where your gate is.*

### Grades 3-5 (Ages 8-11)
**Goal:** Introduce basic phishing, social media basics, and early AI awareness.

| Topic | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Phishing Basics** | Spot a fake email or message | "Real vs. Fake" worksheet with school newsletter examples |
| **Social Media Traps** | Understand likes, comments, and comparison | "The Highlight Reel" — social media shows only the best moments |
| **Password Power** | Create stronger passwords | Passphrase building exercise (e.g., "BlueTiger$RunsFast!") |
| **Intro to AI** | What is a computer making things? | Compare AI-generated art vs. human-made art; discuss prompts |
| **AI Data Privacy** | Understand that AI "remembers" information given to it | "AI Memory Box" activity — what you share stays shared |
| **The "Too Good to Be True" Rule** | Identify basic scams | Story-based learning: free Robux, free Fortnite skins, etc. |
| **Ethical AI Guardrails** | Recognise when AI makes mistakes or is biased | Spot the difference activity — biased AI images vs. real photos |

**AI Literacy Integration (AI Literacy For All Curriculum):**
- **Planting Seeds of AI Basics**: How models learn, work, and appear in daily tools
- **Data Privacy Fundamentals**: Understanding what information AI tools collect
- **Indigenous Data Sovereignty**: How different communities want their data treated

**Key Takeaway:** *Not everything online is real — and that's okay if you know how to check.*

## 📚 Phase 2: Middle School (Grades 6-8)
### Theme: "Own Your Space: Privacy, AI & Digital Self-Defense"

**Goal:** Hands-on skills for account security, social engineering, AI verification, and digital coercion.

| Pillar | Focus Area | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Pillar 1: Account Fortification & Scams** | Phishing via Discord/Roblox/Snapchat; account takeovers via fake login links | Understand credential stuffing and password reuse | Tour a password manager using fictional accounts; practise identifying fake login pages |
| **Pillar 2: AI & Synthetic Media** | Voice cloning for prank calls or "freemium" game currency scams; synthetic imagery | Learn that ears and eyes cannot reliably detect current synthetic media; verify through a second channel | Draft family safe words; run synthetic voice clips and reveal that the class cannot reliably tell |
| **Pillar 3: AI Data Privacy & Ethics** | AI tools collecting data; ethical guardrails; Indigenous data sovereignty | Understand what data AI tools collect and how it is used | Audit AI app permissions; create a personal data privacy plan |
| **Pillar 4: Sextortion & Digital Coercion** | Stranger danger in gaming chats; moving chats to unmonitored secondary apps (Snap/Insta) | Master the "3 Do Nots + Tell" protocol | Role-play sextortion scenarios with discreet reporting |

**Key Protocols Taught:**
- 🔒 **The 3 Do Nots + Tell (sextortion):**
  1. Do NOT pay or negotiate — paying proves you will pay again and never ends the demands
  2. Do NOT reply — any reply feeds them; screenshot, then block
  3. Do NOT delete — screenshots are the evidence police and takedown services need
  4. **TELL** a trusted adult, then report — Cybertip.ca for online sexual exploitation of children, Take It Down if images are involved, 9-1-1 if anyone is in immediate danger
- 🤖 **Synthetic media: provenance first.** Visual and audio "tells" are clues, not proof — current generators make fewer errors every year. Verify by source, reverse image search, and corroboration before you act on what an image or voice claims.
- 🧭 **PAUSE → VERIFY → ACT** — the general decision habit, applied to links, requests, and money.

**AI Literacy For All Resources:**
- **The Clone Game:** voice-clone verification exercise — the point is the safe word and the call-back, not hearing the difference
- **Image Forensic Checks:** basic visual analysis, taught explicitly as clues rather than proof
- **Family Safe Word:** offline verification method for voice clones
- **Indigenous Data Sovereignty:** understanding how different communities govern their digital data

**ISED National AI Strategy Integration:**
- **Canada's AI Map:** locate and explore Mila, Vector, and Amii research institutes
- **Responsible AI Canada:** trust, safety, and accountability frameworks applied to synthetic media
- **AI for All Accessibility:** understanding the digital divide in AI access (urban vs. rural, multilingual tools)

**Silent & Discreet Resources:**
- Take It Down (takeitdown.ncmec.org) — anonymous tool to stop explicit images
- NeedHelpNow.ca — confidential guidance on image sharing and cyberbullying
- **Kids Help Phone — 1-800-668-6868**, and **9-8-8** Suicide Crisis Helpline — 24/7 crisis support
- **Cybertip.ca — 1-866-658-9022** — reporting line for the online sexual exploitation of children, operated by the Canadian Centre for Child Protection. Staffed Monday–Friday, 8:30 a.m.–4:00 p.m. Central Time. **It is not a 24/7 line: if a child is in immediate danger, call 9-1-1.**
- AI Literacy For All: educator module for AI basics and synthetic media

**Key Takeaway:** *Your digital self is yours to protect. Know your boundaries, lock your accounts, verify before you trust, and never be afraid to ask for help.*

## 📚 Phase 3: High School (Grades 9-12)
### Theme: "The Wild West: Con Artists, Crypto, AI & Legal Landscapes"

**Goal:** Advanced threat assessment, AI literacy, financial literacy online, legal understanding, and real-world implementation.

| Pillar | Focus Area | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Pillar 1: Account Fortification & Scams** | Credential stuffing; password reuse across university/job portals; fake brand/collab offers | Understand multi-factor authentication (MFA), password managers, and why passkeys resist phishing | Set up app-based MFA; audit personal email for reused passwords; compare SMS, TOTP, and passkeys |
| **Pillar 2: AI & Synthetic Media** | Voice clones targeting parents (fake emergency/bail calls); visual deepfake manipulation; audio forensics | Trace provenance (original source, uploader history, reverse search) and treat forensic artefacts as supporting clues only | Provenance-first investigation with a public detector demo used as a prompt to check, never as a verdict |
| **Pillar 3: AI Data Privacy & Indigenous Sovereignty** | AI data collection practices; ethical guardrails; Indigenous data governance frameworks | Evaluate AI tools against privacy standards and Indigenous data principles | Personal AI audit: track what data your apps collect and propose governance rules |
| **Pillar 4: Sextortion & Digital Coercion** | Coercive control; financial sextortion rings (Snapchat/Instagram DMs); non-consensual image sharing; AI-enhanced social engineering | Understand the business model behind sextortion syndicates; know the legal response options | Analyse documented case patterns; map each step to an offence and to an exit point |
| **Pillar 5: Crypto Scams** | NFT drops, "rug pulls," airdrop scams, fake exchange promotions, "recovery agents" | Distinguish legitimate crypto projects from pump-and-dump schemes and second-layer scams | Simulated crypto investment exercise with loss/gain tracking |
| **Pillar 6: Social Media Traps (Advanced)** | Algorithmic echo chambers, engagement bait, influencer affiliate traps | Understand how platforms monetise attention and how AI-curated feeds amplify | Audit personal social media feed for ad patterns; algorithm transparency report |
| **Pillar 7: Legal AI & Canadian Law** | Criminal Code: extortion s.346, publication of intimate images s.162.1, fraud s.380, identity theft s.402.2; consent in digital space | Navigate the legal landscape for AI-driven crimes | Guest panel (RCMP/IT forensics/lawyer); multi-platform incident response simulation |

**Goal:** Introduce personal cybersecurity planning, information integrity, and legal awareness.

| Topic | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Account Security Assessment** | Evaluate the strength of online accounts | Practise with fictional persona accounts |
| **Authentication Strength** | Compare different authentication methods | Evaluate SMS, TOTP, passkeys, and security keys |
| **Phishing-Resistant Authentication** | Understand passkeys and security keys | Hands-on introduction on demo accounts |
| **Recovery Channel Protection** | Secure account recovery information | Practise protecting recovery codes |
| **Device Encryption** | Understand encryption concepts | Explore how encryption protects data |

| **Financial Scams** | Identify common financial fraud patterns | Analyse documented examples |
| **Canadian Legal Awareness** | Understand legal responsibilities | Review Criminal Code s.162.1 for intimate image publication |
| **Media Provenance** | Evaluate the source and authenticity of digital content | Practise source tracing and corroboration |

#### Grades 11-12 (Ages 16-18) — Digital Independence
**Goal:** Develop advanced cybersecurity thinking and ethical decision-making.

| Topic | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Threat Modelling** | Assess digital risks and vulnerabilities | Apply threat modelling to personal and organisational scenarios |
| **Defence in Depth** | Understand layered security concepts | Design a security plan with multiple controls |
| **Access Control** | Learn about permissions and data protection | Evaluate application and network permissions |
| **Data States** | Understand data at rest, in transit, and in use | Explore how data is protected in different states |
| **Cloud and IoT Risks** | Identify risks associated with connected devices | Analyse the security of smart-home devices |
| **Backup and Recovery** | Plan for data protection and recovery | Develop a personal recovery strategy |
| **Incident Response** | Learn how to respond to security incidents | Participate in a tabletop exercise |
| **Ethical Cybersecurity** | Understand professional ethics and boundaries | Discuss responsible disclosure and acceptable use |
| **Algorithmic Influence** | Understand how algorithms shape information | Analyse algorithmic bias and influence |

**Key Protocols Taught:**
- 🔍 **Provenance Checklist:** Where did this come from? · Does an independent source show the same thing? · What does the original uploader's history look like? · Which forensic clues are present, and what else could explain them?
- 🤖 **AI Data Privacy Audit:** track what data your AI tools collect; apply ethical guardrails
- 🇨🇦 **Legal AI Response:** apply Canadian Criminal Code sections; understand digital consent; know victim rights and reporting routes
- 💰 **Crypto Scam Red Flags:** "guaranteed returns," pressure to act fast, unknown wallets, too-good-to-be-true airdrops, anyone offering to recover your losses for a fee
- 📱 **Social Media Hygiene:** privacy settings audit, tag review, location tagging habits, follower growth vs. engagement rate analysis
- 🌍 **Indigenous Data Sovereignty in AI:** understand different data governance models; propose community AI principles

**AI Literacy For All Resources Integrated:**
- **Cross-Curricular AI Integration:** AI in Math, Science, English, History, Art applications
- **Pedagogical Agency:** student-led AI projects and teaching sessions
- **Community Leadership:** AI literacy outreach presentations
- **Indigenous AI Principles:** reflection on data sovereignty and community governance

**ISED National AI Strategy — AI for All Integration:**
- **National AI Policy Debate:** research Canada's *six*-pillar AI strategy (Protecting Canadians; Empowering Canadians; Powering AI adoption; Building the sovereign AI foundation; Scaling Canadian champions; Trusted partnerships) and present on one
- **AI Literacy Gap Analysis:** examine the strategy's finding that Canada ranks 44th of 47 countries on AI training and literacy — and who locally has and has not had access
- **Canadian AI Careers:** explore roles in AI safety, evaluation, policy, accessibility, and research (Mila, Vector, Amii)
- **AI for All Challenge:** design an AI tool that serves an underserved community, then place it under the pillar it belongs to
- **Transparency and Watermarking Audit:** examine the strategy's commitments on labelling AI-generated content and identify where real-world labelling is absent

**Key Takeaway:** *The internet rewards the informed and punishes the impulsive. Think before you click, invest, share, or trust AI content. Your data is yours — govern it wisely.*

## 🧼 Core Cyber Hygiene Topics

These are not a separate unit. They are woven into the grade bands above and revisited each year, because they are the controls most people never configure.

| Topic | Why it is taught | Where it lands |
|-------|------------------|----------------|
| **Software updates** | Most exploited vulnerabilities have a patch available | All bands; explicit in 6-8 |
| **Device locking and loss** | An unlocked phone is an unlocked account | K-2 (routine), 3-5, 6-8 |
| **Safe downloads** | Sideloaded apps are the main malware route on phones | 3-5, 6-8 |
| **Application permissions** | Access is granted once and forgotten | 3-5, 6-8, 9-12 |
| **Public and shared networks** | Unencrypted traffic is readable on the same network | 6-8 (Wi-Fi Whisperer), 9-12 |
| **Backups** | Ransomware and device loss are survivable with a backup | 6-8, 9-12 |
| **Malware** | Recognising rather than removing; removal is an adult/IT task | 3-5, 6-8 |
| **Account recovery** | Recovery channels bypass every other control | 6-8, 9-12 |
| **Data protection** | What is collected, by whom, and what it can infer | 3-5 onward |
| **Incident recovery** | What to do after, not only during | 6-8, 9-12 |

## 🔐 Authentication Guidance

### Modern best practices

TOTP and SMS are better than a password alone, but **neither is phishing-resistant**. A student who learns "turn on 2FA" and stops there has learned half a control. The progression below matches the sessions in Programs 1-3.

| Method | Strength | Teach it as |
|--------|----------|-------------|
| Password only | Weak — reuse turns one breach into many | The baseline everyone starts from |
| SMS code | Better than nothing; vulnerable to SIM swap and interception | A first step, not the destination |
| Authenticator app (TOTP) | Strong against password theft; still phishable in real time | The practical default for Grades 6-12 |
| **Passkeys / FIDO2** | **Phishing-resistant** — the credential is bound to the legitimate domain | The direction of travel; introduce from Grades 6-8, practise in 9-12 |
| **Security keys** | **Phishing-resistant** hardware | Where a school or family has them |
| **Recovery codes / channels** | Bypasses every control above if left unsecured | Part of the same lesson, not an afterthought |

### Phishing-resistant authentication, explained simply

A password or a six-digit code can be typed into a fake page. A passkey cannot: it is bound to the real domain, so it simply will not offer itself to `r0blox-login.com`. That is the whole argument, and it is teachable to a Grade 8 student in one sentence.

### What this framework does not do

It does not require students to configure authentication on personal accounts during class. Labs run on demo or school-managed accounts, with a no-lab version that sends the setup home as a family mission. See [Safety Guidelines](#safety-guidelines).

## 🤖 Synthetic Media and Misinformation

### The order of checks

Taught in this order, strongest first:

1. **Source** — who published this, on what account, and can that account be verified?
2. **Reverse image search** — does the image or clip appear earlier, elsewhere, in another context?
3. **Corroboration** — does any independent outlet report the same event?
4. **Forensic artefacts** — hand anatomy, lighting and shadow, eye reflections, hair strands, background distortion, lip-sync drift, unnatural breathing.

Step 4 is last on purpose. Artefacts are the weakest check: generators improve every year, compression and real photographs produce the same "clues," and a missing metadata field proves nothing — most platforms strip EXIF on upload.

### What students are taught to say

> "I can't tell from looking. Let me find out where it came from."

That sentence is the outcome of the whole strand. It is honest, it is actionable, and it does not become wrong when the next model ships.

### Detection tools

Public detectors are probabilistic. They produce false positives and false negatives, and they cannot explain themselves. A "94% AI" score is a prompt to check provenance, not a verdict. Where a detector is used in class, the lesson is about its limits as much as its output — and the session runs without one if none is available.

## ⚖️ Canadian Legal Framework

### Publication of intimate images without consent — Criminal Code s.162.1

Section 162.1 is the offence that matters here, **not s.276** (which governs the admissibility of evidence about a complainant's sexual activity — a trial-evidence rule, not a publication offence).

| Provision | What it covers |
|-----------|----------------|
| **s.162.1(1)** | Knowingly publishing, distributing, transmitting, selling, making available, or advertising an intimate image without consent (or being reckless as to consent) |
| **s.162.1(1.1)** | **Threatening** to do so, with intent to intimidate or be taken seriously |
| **s.162.1(2)(a)** | "Intimate image" — a visual recording where the person is nude, nearly nude, or engaged in explicit sexual activity, with a reasonable expectation of privacy |
| **s.162.1(2)(b)** | "Intimate image" **also includes a visual representation made by any electronic or mechanical means, including by means of artificial intelligence software**, showing an identifiable person depicted that way, if the depiction is likely to be mistaken for a real recording |

The AI provision matters for this curriculum: a synthetic image of a real classmate is not a loophole. It is the same offence.

Related offences worth naming for Grades 9-12:

- **Extortion — s.346** — the charge that fits the "pay me or I'll post it" demand
- **Fraud — s.380** — the charge that fits the payment itself
- **Identity theft and identity fraud — s.402.2 and s.403**
- **Child sexual abuse material provisions** — for cases involving minors, discussed at pattern level only, never with images

### Sextortion awareness

Students should understand that:

- Paying does not stop the demands; it confirms the victim will pay, and the demands escalate
- The response is the **3 Do Nots + Tell** — don't pay, don't reply, don't delete, tell a trusted adult — followed by reporting
- Screenshots are evidence, and takedown services need them
- Support and reporting routes are Cybertip.ca (online sexual exploitation of children), Take It Down (image removal), NeedHelpNow.ca, Kids Help Phone, and 9-1-1 in an emergency
- Being a victim is not an offence and not a disciplinary matter in this framework; the protocol is designed so a student can follow it while frightened

> ⚠️ **Not legal advice.** This section is a teaching summary. Boards should have their own legal counsel review any materials that describe offences, and staff should follow board policy on mandatory reporting.

## 🛡️ Safety Guidelines

### Practice without personal exposure

Classroom exercises use:

- Fictional personas
- Demo or school-managed accounts
- Synthetic messages and sample credential dumps
- Sandbox environments (VMs, local servers, offline forms)
- **Never** real passwords, real credentials, or real personal information

A useful test before any activity: *if a student's screenshot of this worksheet leaked, would anyone be harmed?* If yes, redesign the activity.

Specifically:

- No student writes a real password on paper, in a document, or into a form. Password-mapping exercises use account *names* only.
- No student records their own voice or a family member's voice for a cloning exercise. Synthetic samples or licensed clips are used instead.
- No student uses a classmate's image, name, or account in a forensic or OSINT exercise.
- Deepfake creation is not an activity in this curriculum. Analysis is.

### Safeguarding protocols

For sensitive topics — sextortion, image-based abuse, self-harm, and exploitation:

- Counsellor or trusted adult presence during the lesson, arranged in advance
- A named, ordered response protocol students can repeat from memory
- Board mandatory-reporting procedures followed, and known to the teacher before the lesson
- Student opt-out available without explanation
- Crisis referral routes visible in the room: 9-1-1, Kids Help Phone **1-800-668-6868**, **9-8-8**
- No requirement to disclose personal experience, aloud or in writing
- A plan for what to do if a student discloses during or after the lesson

## 🪜 Curriculum Progression

The autonomy ladder gives each grade band a decision cycle it can actually run. Each step is one more move than the band before it.

| Band | Decision cycle |
|------|----------------|
| **K-2** | STOP → ASK AN ADULT |
| **Grades 3-5** | PAUSE → CHECK → ASK → REPORT |
| **Grades 6-8** | PAUSE → VERIFY → PROTECT → REPORT |
| **Grades 9-10** | ASSESS → VERIFY → PROTECT → RECOVER |
| **Grades 11-12** | ASSESS RISK → SELECT CONTROLS → RESPOND → RECOVER → IMPROVE |

The ladder is deliberately cumulative: "assess" contains "pause," and "improve" contains "recover." A Grade 12 student who has forgotten the vocabulary can still be asked *"what would you have done in Grade 2?"* and the answer is still correct, just incomplete.

## 📅 Suggested Delivery Schedule

### Option A: Monthly "Cyber Safety Moments" (recommended)
- **15-minute assemblies or classroom sessions** once per month
- Rotating themes throughout the school year
- Aligned with Cybersecurity Awareness Month (October) for the full campaign push

### Option B: Dedicated "Digital Literacy" period
- Integrated into the weekly schedule (30-45 min/week)
- More comprehensive coverage, but requires curriculum allocation

### Option C: Parent-student joint workshops
- Quarterly sessions where families learn together
- Builds home-school consistency, which matters most for younger students

## 📊 Assessment and Evaluation

### Knowledge checks by band

| Grade Band | Assessment Method |
|-----------|------------------|
| **K-2** | Visual recognition (point to the safe link); oral questioning; AI comparison activities |
| **3-5** | Worksheet completion; group role-play; AI data privacy reflection |
| **6-8** | Practical lab (set up MFA on a demo account, identify a fake login page); scenario-based multiple choice; media provenance checks |
| **9-12** | Project-based assessment (audit an account's privacy settings); written analysis of a real AI scam case; legal application; evidence brief with a stated confidence level |

### Transfer-based assessment

Assessments measure transfer, not disclosure. A student should never have to reveal a real password, a real incident, or a family situation in order to be graded.

Preferred evidence:

- Sandbox demonstrations
- Scenario explanations and decision trees
- Teach-backs (student explains the protocol to a partner or a younger class)
- Anonymised portfolios
- Written evidence briefs that state what the evidence does *not* prove

### AI literacy progression milestones

- ✅ **Elementary (K-5):**
  - Can identify safe vs. unsafe links
  - Knows password basics
  - Understands the digital footprint concept
  - Can describe what AI is in simple terms ("a computer that learns from examples")
  - Understands that AI "remembers" information given to it
  - Recognises when AI makes mistakes or shows bias

- ✅ **Middle School (6-8):**
  - Can set up MFA on a demo account
  - Identifies phishing messages
  - Can state the 3 Do Nots + Tell from memory
  - Recognises that synthetic media can look flawless, and verifies surprising content by source before sharing
  - Audits AI app permissions and data collection
  - Applies Indigenous data sovereignty principles to personal AI use
  - Uses the Family Safe Word and a call-back for voice verification

- ✅ **High School (9-12):**
  - Can audit accounts for security, including recovery channels
  - Assesses whether media can be trusted by tracing provenance, and states the limits of the evidence
  - Evaluates crypto scams and recognises second-layer "recovery" scams
  - Explains algorithmic bias and engagement incentives on social media
  - Analyses AI data collection against ethical guardrails
  - Applies Canadian Criminal Code sections to AI-driven crimes
  - Proposes community AI governance principles based on Indigenous data sovereignty
  - Leads cross-curricular AI projects

## 💡 Why This Framework Works

1. **Progressive complexity:** concepts build year over year, reinforcing and expanding prior knowledge
2. **Age-appropriate language:** no jargon without explanation; metaphors carry the concept
3. **Action-oriented:** every lesson ends with a skill the student can use immediately
4. **Discreet and judgment-free:** the sextortion protocol avoids shame and keeps the student's exit open
5. **Future-proof by construction:** the durable skill is provenance and procedure, not detector knowledge that expires
6. **Family-centric:** extends learning home through parent guides and shared activities
7. **One protocol, everywhere:** the same words in the classroom, the handout, the parent toolkit, and the assessment

## 🛑 The Stop and Think Principle — Our North Star

Every lesson, every phase, and every activity reinforces one core habit: **STOP → THINK → ASK / TAKE A BREAK**

This principle is developmentally adaptive:

- **K-2:** "Red means stop, yellow means think, green means go!"
- **Grades 3-5:** "Pause before you pounce! Great detectives don't rush!"
- **Grades 6-8:** "Own your space — analyse before you act! Your worth isn't tied to a notification!"
- **Grades 9-12:** "Never let urgency decide for you! The best digital defence is a well-rested brain!"

**Every student learns:**

- ✅ **STOP** before clicking, sending, sharing, or spending
- ✅ **THINK** — "Does this make sense? Who is this from? What happens if I act?"
- ✅ **ASK** a trusted person if unsure — *"If you struggle at any time with any content, ask for help. There is no wrong question."*
- ✅ **TAKE A BREAK** if overwhelmed — *"If your brain feels buzzy, step away. Come back fresh. The screen will still be there."*

📖 **Full Stop & Think guide:** see [Stop_Think_Reinforcement.md](Stop_Think_Reinforcement.md)
