## 🗺️ Framework Overview

This progressive education framework takes students from foundational digital literacy in Kindergarten through advanced AI literacy and threat detection in Grade 12. It integrates government-backed AI education resources with practical digital self-defense skills, covering both **legacy threats** (phishing, social media traps) and **emerging AI-driven threats** (deepfakes, voice cloning, synthetic media, crypto scams) in age-appropriate ways.

The "Wild West" metaphor helps students understand that the internet is a place of opportunity but also requires protection, awareness, and smart decision-making — much like frontier towns needed both cowboys and sheriffs.

### 🔄 New AI Education Integration (v2.0 Updates)

This framework now incorporates two major AI literacy resources:

1. **ISED AI Literacy Initiative** — Foundational knowledge about how AI models learn, work, and integrate into daily tools; data privacy fundamentals; ethical guardrails
2. **AI Literacy For All Curriculum** — Pedagogical agency for educators; Indigenous data sovereignty; cross-curricular AI applications; self-paced educator modules
3. **ISED Canada's National AI Strategy — AI for All** — Pan-Canadian AI research strategy; responsible AI development; AI adoption across sectors; Indigenous AI & data sovereignty; AI skills and talent development; global leadership

**Key Additions:**
- Progressive AI curriculum from "What is AI?" (K-2) through "Audio Forensics & Legal AI" (Grades 9-12)
- Indigenous data sovereignty woven through all grade bands
- Ethical AI guardrails and data privacy as cross-cutting themes
- Educator development track via AI Literacy For All self-paced modules
- Cross-curricular AI integration across all subjects

## 📚 PHASE 1: Elementary (K-5)
### Theme: "My Digital Body & Online Space"

#### Kindergarten - Grade 2 (Ages 5-8)
**Goal:** Build foundational awareness of online safety through storytelling and routine habits.

| Topic | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Who am I talking to?** | Identify familiar vs. unfamiliar people online | "Stranger Danger" digital version with cartoon characters |
| **The Password Lock** | Understand passwords as keys to your door | Create a physical "password lock" craft; practice saying passwords out loud (using fictional examples only) (using fictional examples only) (not sharing) |
| **Clicking Safely** | Distinguish safe links vs. suspicious links | Game: "Tap the Green Button, Not the Red One" |
| **Screen Time Boundaries** | Recognize healthy tech habits | Family screen-time contract; visual timer activities |
| **Digital Footprint** | Understand that online actions stay forever | "Footprints in the Sand" analogy — what disappears vs. what stays |

**AI Literacy Integration:**
- **What is a Computer Making?**: Introduce AI as a "helper that learns from examples" using picture books and comparison activities
- **Indigenous Storytelling Connection**: Use traditional storytelling patterns to explain how AI "learns" from stories and patterns it has seen

**Key Takeaway:** *The internet is like a playground — you can have fun, but always know who's watching and where your gate is.*

#### Grades 3-5 (Ages 8-11)
**Goal:** Introduce basic phishing, social media basics, and early AI awareness.

| Topic | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Phishing Basics** | Spot a fake email or message | "Real vs. Fake" worksheet with school newsletter examples |
| **Social Media Traps** | Understand likes, comments, and comparison | "The Highlight Reel" — social media shows only the best moments |
| **Password Power** | Create stronger passwords | Passphrase building exercise (e.g., "BlueTiger$RunsFast!") |
| **Intro to AI** | What is a computer making things? | Compare AI-generated art vs. human-made art; discuss prompts |
| **AI Data Privacy** | Understand that AI "remembers" information given to it | "AI Memory Box" activity — what you share stays shared |
| **The "Too Good to Be True" Rule** | Identify basic scams | Story-based learning: free Robux, free Fortnite skins, etc. |
| **Ethical AI Guardrails** | Recognize when AI makes mistakes or biases | Spot the difference activity — biased AI images vs. real photos |

**AI Literacy Integration (AI Literacy For All Curriculum):**
- **Planting Seeds of AI Basics**: How models learn, work, and sprout in daily tools
- **Data Privacy Fundamentals**: Understanding what information AI tools collect
- **Indigenous Data Sovereignty**: How different communities want their data treated

**Key Takeaway:** *Not everything online is real — and that's okay if you know how to check.*

## 📚 PHASE 2: Middle School (Grades 6-8)
### Theme: "Own Your Space: Privacy, AI & Digital Self-Defense"

#### Grades 6-8 (Ages 11-14)
**Goal:** Hands-on skills for account security, social engineering, AI detection, and digital coercion.

| Pillar | Focus Area | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Pillar 1: Account Fortification & Scams** | Phishing via Discord/Roblox/Snapchat; account takeovers via fake login links | Understand credential stuffing and password reuse | Create a password manager; practice identifying fake login pages |
| **Pillar 2: AI & Synthetic Media** | Voice cloning for prank calls or "freemium" game currency scams; spotting synthetic imagery | Learn the "Family Safe Word" to counter voice clones | Record family safe words; play AI voice clone audio samples |
| **Pillar 3: AI Data Privacy & Ethics** | AI tools collecting data; ethical guardrails; Indigenous data sovereignty | Understand what data AI tools collect and how it's used | Audit AI app permissions; create a personal data privacy plan |
| **Pillar 4: Sextortion & Digital Coercion** | Stranger danger in gaming chats; moving chats to unmonitored secondary apps (Snap/Insta) | Master "Stop, Screenshot, Block, Report" protocol | Role-play sextortion scenarios with discreet reporting |

**Key Protocols Taught:**
- 🔒 **The 3 Do Nots of Sextortion:**
  1. Do NOT pay or negotiate (paying always leads to more demands)
  2. Do NOT delete evidence immediately (screenshot first!)
  3. Do NOT panic alone (loop in a trusted adult)
- 🤖 **AI Detection Basics:** Check for unnatural patterns, inconsistent lighting, mismatched details in AI images; listen for robotic speech patterns in audio

**AI Literacy For All Resources:**
- **The Clone Game:** Voice cloning detection exercise
- **Image Forensic Checks:** Basic visual analysis techniques
- **Family Safe Word:** Offline verification method for voice clones
- **Indigenous Data Sovereignty:** Understanding how different communities govern their digital data

**ISED National AI Strategy Integration:**
- **Canada's AI Map:** Locate and explore Mila, Vector, Amii research institutes
- **Responsible AI Canada:** Trust, safety, and accountability frameworks applied to synthetic media
- **AI for All Accessibility:** Understanding the digital divide in AI access (urban vs. rural, multilingual tools)

**Silent & Discreet Resources:**
- Take It Down (takeitdown.ncmec.org) — Anonymous tool to stop explicit images
- NeedHelpNow.ca — Confidential guidance on image sharing and cyberbullying
- Kids Help Phone / Cybertip.ca — 24/7 crisis support lines
- AI Literacy For All: Educator module for AI basics and synthetic media

**Key Takeaway:** *Your digital self is yours to protect. Know your boundaries, lock your accounts, verify AI content, and never be afraid to ask for help.*

## 📚 PHASE 3: High School (Grades 9-12)
### Theme: "The Wild West: Con Artists, Crypto, AI & Legal Landscapes"

#### Grades 9-12 (Ages 14-18)
**Goal:** Advanced threat detection, AI literacy, financial literacy online, legal applications, and real-world implementation.

| Pillar | Focus Area | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Pillar 1: Account Fortification & Scams** | Credential stuffing; password reuse across university/job portals; fake brand/collab offers | Understand multi-factor authentication (MFA) and password managers | Set up app-based 2FA; audit personal email for reused passwords |
| **Pillar 2: AI & Synthetic Media** | Hyper-realistic voice clones targeting parents (fake emergency/bail calls); visual deepfake manipulation; audio forensics | Learn forensic analysis (EXIF data, lighting, lip-sync errors, spectral analysis) | Deepfake detection workshop using free tools; audio forensics lab |
| **Pillar 3: AI Data Privacy & Indigenous Sovereignty** | AI data collection practices; ethical guardrails; Indigenous data governance frameworks | Evaluate AI tools against privacy standards and Indigenous data principles | Personal AI audit: track what data your apps collect and propose governance rules |
| **Pillar 4: Sextortion & Digital Coercion** | Coercive control; financial sextortion rings (Snapchat/Instagram DMs); non-consensual image sharing; AI-enhanced social engineering | Understand the business model behind sextortion syndicates; legal response options | Analyze real case studies from Cybertip.ca; apply Canadian Criminal Code sections |
| **Pillar 5: Crypto Scams** | NFT drops, "rug pulls," airdrop scams, fake exchange promotions | Distinguish legitimate crypto projects from pump-and-dump schemes | Simulated crypto investment exercise with loss/gain tracking |
| **Pillar 6: Social Media Traps (Advanced)** | Algorithmic echo chambers, engagement bait, influencer affiliate traps | Understand how platforms monetize attention; AI-curated feeds | Audit personal social media feed for ad patterns; algorithm transparency report |
| **Pillar 7: Legal AI & Canadian Law** | Criminal Code: extortion s.346, publication of intimate images s.276, fraud, identity theft; consent in digital space | Navigate legal landscape for AI-driven crimes | Guest panel (RCMP/IT forensics/lawyer); multi-platform incident response simulation |

**Key Protocols Taught:**
- 🔍 **AI Detection Checklist:** Check for unnatural hand proportions, inconsistent lighting, mismatched shadows, audio lip-sync errors, spectral analysis for voice clones
- 🤖 **AI Data Privacy Audit:** Track what data your AI tools collect; apply ethical guardrails
- 🇨🇦 **Legal AI Response:** Apply Canadian Criminal Code sections; understand digital consent; navigate victim rights
- 💰 **Crypto Scam Red Flags:** "Guaranteed returns," pressure to act fast, unknown wallets, too-good-to-be-true airdrops
- 📱 **Social Media Hygiene:** Privacy settings audit, tag review, location tagging habits, follower growth vs. engagement rate analysis
- 🌍 **Indigenous Data Sovereignty in AI:** Understand different data governance models; propose community AI principles

**AI Literacy For All Resources Integrated:**
- **Cross-Curricular AI Integration:** AI in Math, Science, English, History, Art applications
- **Pedagogical Agency:** Student-led AI projects and teaching sessions
- **Community Leadership:** AI literacy outreach presentations
- **Indigenous AI Principles:** Reflection on data sovereignty and community governance

**ISED National AI Strategy — AI for All Integration:**
- **National AI Policy Debate:** Research Canada's 5-pillar AI strategy and present on one pillar
- **AIDA (AI & Data Act):** Understand Canada's proposed AI legislation and its impact on students
- **Canadian AI Careers:** Explore careers in AI research, policy, ethics, and development
- **AI for All Challenge:** Design an AI tool that serves an underserved community
- **Canada.AI Transparency:** Analyze how the federal government uses AI in public services

**Key Takeaway:** *The internet rewards the informed and punishes the impulsive. Think before you click, invest, share, or trust AI content. Your data is yours — govern it wisely.*

## 💡 Why This Framework Works

1. **Progressive Complexity:** Concepts build year-over-year, reinforcing and expanding prior knowledge
2. **Age-Appropriate Language:** No jargon without explanation; metaphors replace technical terms where needed
3. **Action-Oriented:** Every lesson ends with a practical skill students can use immediately
4. **Discreet & Judgment-Free:** Especially for sextortion, protocols avoid shame and embarrassment
5. **Future-Proof:** Designed to adapt as new threats emerge (add pillars without replacing existing ones)
6. **Family-Centric:** Extends learning home through parent guides and shared activities
7. **Stop & Think Core Principle:** At every level, students learn to PAUSE before acting online — the single most important cyber safety habit

## 🛑 The Stop & Think Principle — Our North Star

Every lesson, every phase, and every activity reinforces one core habit: **STOP → THINK → ASK / TAKE A BREAK**

This principle is developmentally adaptive:
- **K-2:** "Red means stop, yellow means think, green means go!"
- **Grades 3-5:** "Pause before you pounce! Great detectives don't rush!"
- **Grades 6-8:** "Own your space — analyze before you act! Your worth isn't tied to a notification!"
- **Grades 9-12:** "Never let urgency decide for you! The best digital defense is a well-rested brain!"

**Every student learns:**
- ✅ **STOP** before clicking, sending, sharing, or spending
- ✅ **THINK** — "Does this make sense? Who is this from? What happens if I act?"
- ✅ **ASK** a trusted person if unsure — *"If you struggle at any time with any content, ask for help. There is no wrong question."*
- ✅ **TAKE A BREAK** if overwhelmed — *"If your brain feels buzzy, step away. Come back fresh. The screen will still be there."*
