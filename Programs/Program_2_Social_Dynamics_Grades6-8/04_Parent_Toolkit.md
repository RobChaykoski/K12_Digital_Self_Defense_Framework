# 👨‍👩‍👧 Program 2 — Parent & Guardian Toolkit (Grades 6–8)

---

## Why This Age Is Different
Your kid is now in the **social pressure zone**. The threats are no longer cartoonish: they're organized scammers, AI clones of real voices and faces, and sextortion operations that run like call centres. And the social cost of "telling" (what will my friends think?) is at its peak. **Your job this program: make telling you the EASIEST and SAFEST option — easier than staying silent.**

## The Three Family Agreements to Make This Program
1. **The No-Blame Promise** (say it out loud, in front of your kid):
   *"If anything online ever makes you uncomfortable, scared, or guilty — you can tell me and I will NOT yell, take your phone, or be mad at YOU. I might be mad at the situation, but my first job is to help you. Deal?"* Sign it together (p.3). **This promise is what makes every other lesson work.**
2. **The Code Phrase** — a normal-sounding sentence that means *"I need to talk to you privately about something online"*: e.g., "Can we get pizza?" / "Where's my charger?" Agree on one this week so your kid can use it even when they can't say it directly.
3. **The Family Safe Word** — a word only your family knows, used to verify any urgent request (voice note, call, text) asking for money or personal info. If the safe word isn't there → don't act, verify another way.

## If Your Child Discloses Something (the most important page in this toolkit)
**Their first 24 hours matter more than any investigation.**
1. **No blame, no anger, no device seizure as punishment.** Say: "Thank you for telling me. This is not your fault. Here's what we'll do."
2. **Preserve evidence:** don't delete the conversations or accounts; screenshot everything (dates, usernames, messages).
3. **The 3 Do Nots apply to them too:** don't pay anything, don't reply to the scammer, don't let them feel they must fix it alone.
4. **Report:** **Cybertip.ca** (confidential, Canada's national tipline — sextortion is one of its top report types). If images are involved: **Take It Down** (NCMEC, anonymous takedown) and **NeedHelpNow.ca** for youth support.
5. **Support over surveillance:** temporary screen-time changes are fine as *care*, framed as "we're resting your eyes/brain", not punishment. Watch for trauma responses (sleep changes, withdrawal, grade drops) — follow up with a counsellor if they persist.
6. **If you suspect your child is on the OTHER side** (pressuring others): this is also a disclosure to handle calmly — it's often peer pressure or curiosity gone wrong. Guidance counsellor + C3P's restorative approaches exist for exactly this; shame guarantees it continues in secret.

## Quick Reference: The Adult's Cheat Sheet
- **Sextortion demand?** → No pay, no reply, screenshot, Cybertip.ca, Take It Down if images. Victim is never at fault.
- **"Is this really [person]?" voice/text asking for money** → safe word; verify by another channel.
- **Shocking video/image of a real person** → assume it *might* be fake until verified; don't share while checking.
- **Account hacked** → password reset + MFA, review what the account held, warn their friends.
- **Phishing link clicked / info typed in** → change that password everywhere it's used, watch for odd activity, breathe — acting fast usually means no damage.
