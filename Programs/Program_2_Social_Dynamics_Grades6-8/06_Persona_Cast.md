# 🎧 Persona Cast: "The Counter-Intelligence Crew" (Grades 6–8)

**Program:** Social Dynamics | **Ages:** 11–14 | **Tone:** Agency, not nursery rhyme

---

## 💡 Design Philosophy — Why This Cast Is Different

Kids in grades 6–8 **cringe at mascots.** An animal in a tool belt is now "baby stuff." But they are *deeply* attracted to: hacking culture, heists and counter-heists, agencies and clearance levels, being "in the know," and the idea of professional skill. So this cast is built from **real offensive/defensive cyber security roles**, translated into a teen agency format:

| Cyber Security Culture (the real thing) | Teen Translation |
|------------------------------------------|------------------|
| White-hat / ethical hacker | "We break things so the bad guys can't" |
| Red team vs. blue team | Attackers vs. defenders — and this crew is the blue team |
| Bug bounty hunters | Getting paid to find flaws before scammers do |
| OSINT (open-source intelligence) | "If it's public, they already have it" |
| MFA / account hardening | Locking down your digital life like a vault door |
| Audio & visual forensics | Proving a voice or video is cloned — with evidence |
| Digital forensics / chain of custody | Preserving evidence so professionals can act (screenshot first, delete never) |
| Incident response / disclosure channels | Discreet, judgment-free ways to get help — the safe word, Quickie Cards, Cybertip.ca |
| Scam syndicates (the real threat model) | **The Glitch Syndicate** — not a cartoon villain, a *business* |

### The Two Rules That Changed From K–5
1. **The characters are people, not mascots.** Teens aged 14–17 (a few years older than the students — aspirational peers, never babies). One squad member is a text-based AI interface, because that's what this age actually lives with.
2. **The villain is an organization, not a character.** This is deliberate pedagogy: sextortion and account-takeover scams are run by *professional criminal businesses* (per the campaign's "organized scheme" framing). A faceless syndicate teaches the real lesson — *"You're not dealing with a person like you. You're dealing with a call center of con artists, and they have a playbook."* It removes shame (it's professionals vs. an 11-year-old) and matches how the Block B sessions must be taught.

### Continuity With the K–5 Cyber Squad
The Cyber Squad doesn't disappear — it becomes **the origin story**:
- The program opens with a one-page "dossier": *Every agent in this crew started as a Little Guardian. Cipher still carries Locky's red-ribbon key on a keychain.*
- **Trickster Bot was the prototype.** His story: one glitchy bot, run by one grifter in a basement. Then it got industrialized — thousands of bots, scripts, cloned voices, fake faces. *"Trickster Bot didn't get smarter. He just got staff."* This gives returning students (who met him in K–5) an instant hook and makes the syndicate feel real and new.
- Captain Shield's Pause Button becomes the crew's motto: **PAUSE → VERIFY → ACT.** Same instinct, upgraded vocabulary.
- **Detective Dot's spotting thread continues through Cipher and Ghost.** Dot's 3 Warning Signs (too good to be true / hurry hurry / asks for your secrets) become the crew's PAUSE → VERIFY → ACT method under Cipher (S1 phishing), and her magnifying glass becomes Ghost's OSINT dossier. Her magnifying-glass icon reappears on the verification checklist — returning students should recognize their childhood tool in the new kit.
- **Bloom's kindness beat lives on through Static.** The safe word and the quiet channel are Bloom's Un-Filter grown up: helping someone without making them perform. S7 includes a 5-minute "Real Thing" highlight-reel moment (see lesson plan), and one line of Bloom's appears on every Quickie Card — so her theme never disappears for two years.

## 🧊 CIPHER — Incident Commander
*(Lead. Appears in every session, fully in S1/S8/S9/S10)*

- **Who:** 16, sharp and calm, black hoodie with a small silver keychain on the pocket — **Locky's red-ribbon key** (the K–5 callback; students who remember it will feel the lineage). Runs everything from a battered laptop stickered with old CTF tournament stickers.
- **Personality:** Never raises their voice, which is somehow more intimidating than if they did. Talks in *playbooks*, not lectures: "Here's what the syndicate does in step one. Here's how we beat it in step one."
- **Superpower (narrative):** **The Playbook** — can pull up any scam scenario and show its anatomy: who runs it, what they want, the exact moment a victim can still walk away.
- **Owns the concept:** **PAUSE → VERIFY → ACT.** The upgraded Pause Button. Also owns the framing that repeats in Block B: *"They are professionals. You're 12. That's not an even fight — and it was never supposed to be one. The protocol is how we make it even."*
- **Catchphrase:** *"Trust nothing. Verify everything. Act anyway."*
- **In lessons:** Opens S1 by "debriefing" the class like agents; runs the S9 full-spectrum drill as the incident commander calling out scenarios; hands out certification in S10. In Block B sessions Cipher's role is *procedure only* — clinical, zero drama, which models exactly how adults should sound when a student discloses something.
- **Design notes:** Androgynous-presenting so any student can see themselves as the lead. The keychain is the most important detail in the whole cast — it must appear in every illustration of Cipher.

## 🔧 PATCH — Security Engineer
*(S2: Lock It Down — Account Fortification & MFA)*

- **Who:** 15, the crew's engineer. Fidgets constantly (fidget spinner that's actually a tiny USB drive). Wears a bandana as a "do not touch my setup" sign. Has a wall of sticky notes that is just passwords crossed out: *sunshine1, password, dragon2013…*
- **Personality:** Genuinely, comically paranoid in the best way — treats a Roblox account with the same seriousness as a bank vault, and can't explain why without getting excited. The only crew member who argues with Cipher (and loses, every time, happily).
- **Superpower:** **The Vault Door** — walks students through hardening an account layer by layer: unique passphrase → MFA → checking where the account is exposed → what to do if it's already compromised. Their sticky-note wall is the teaching prop: *"Every single one of these got someone hacked. Yours could be next week."*
- **Owns the concept:** Passphrases + multi-factor authentication, taught as *skill proof* — every student leaves S2 having enabled MFA on at least one real account (school-managed demo accounts where devices are banned).
- **Catchphrase:** *"A password is a lock. MFA is the vault door. You're carrying a screen door."*
- **In lessons:** Stars S2 end-to-end; returns briefly in S9's drill as the "account already compromised" scenario handler. Their sticky-note wall becomes a printable classroom poster: *Famous Weak Passwords Hall of Shame.*
- **Design notes:** The crossed-out sticky notes are the character — if the art shows Patch without them, it's wrong. Make the USB fidget spinner a recurring gag (it appears in every panel).

## 🎙️ ECHO — Audio Forensics Analyst
*(S3: The Clone Game — Voice Cloning & AI Audio)*

- **Who:** 17, the crew's oldest and its quietest. Lives half the time with headphones on. Desk is a tangle of cables and one very good microphone they refuse to let anyone touch ("It's calibrated. Don't.")
- **Personality:** Speaks in short sentences; when they're excited it's terrifying because you can barely tell. Has a running bit: they will *always* ask a new person their "verification word" as a joke — and then, in S3, reveal that was the whole lesson.
- **Superpower:** **The Waveform** — can look at any audio clip (on a big screen) and show the tells of a clone: too-perfect pitch, weird breaths (or none), timing that's slightly robotic, words said *almost* right.
- **Owns the concept:** Voice cloning — including the specific threat from the campaign: cloned voices used in prank calls and "freemium" game-currency scams, and (for the family mission) the fake-emergency call targeting parents. Owns **the Family Safe Word** as a countermeasure: an offline phrase only your real family knows, that no clone can guess.
- **Catchphrase:** *"If it sounds perfect… ask the question only the real them could answer."*
- **In lessons:** Stars S3 — runs the live demo (a clearly-labelled cloned clip of a cartoon character), teaches the waveform tells, and leads the class in writing their family safe word. The "verification word" bit pays off: students realize they've been practicing the defence since the hook.
- **Design notes:** Headphones are the logo. Echo is always shown *listening*, not talking — that's the whole personality in one pose.

## 📸 LENS — Visual Forensics Analyst
*(S4: Spot the Fake — Visual Deepfakes & AI Images)*

- **Who:** 15, part photographer, part skeptic. Carries a camera everywhere and has a phone folder called "caught in 4K" full of fakes they've spotted and dissected. Wears one airpud at all times ("for calibration").
- **Personality:** Fast-talking, slightly show-offy — but the class quickly learns their shows are *always right*, which earns them respect. Has a rule they tattoo on every worksheet: **"Surprising + Unverified = Not shared."**
- **Superpower:** **The Zoom** — can take any image and walk through the forensic checklist with it: hands and fingers, teeth, text that's slightly wrong, lighting that doesn't match, edges around faces, reflections that lie.
- **Owns the concept:** Visual deepfakes & AI images — at least 3 tells, plus the *behavioural* skill that matters more than any tell: **verify surprising media before you share it** (DM the person, check a second source). Also co-stars with Ghost on "how syndicates harvest your own photos to build fake profiles."
- **Catchphrase:** *"Zoom in. Lies have edges."*
- **In lessons:** Stars S4 — runs the Real-or-Robot image sort (upgraded from Track B's Pixel game: these are *harder*, closer to real), teaches the "caught in 4K" dissection method, and sets the verify-before-sharing rule that recurs in S9's drill.
- **Design notes:** Camera strap is always on; one airpud always visible. Their aesthetic is "street photographer," not "tech bro" — denim, canvas, scuffed boots.

## 🗄️ VAULT — Digital Forensics / Chain of Custody
*(S5, S6, S8 — the Block B anchor)*

- **Who:** 16, the crew's calmest person and the one students in crisis situations respond to most. Short sentences. Never flinches. Keeps a real evidence bag and evidence tags on their desk as props, and treats them with total seriousness.
- **Personality:** The reason this character exists: in Block B, kids need an adult-coded figure who is **boring about the scary stuff.** Vault explains sextortion the way someone explains a fire drill — steps, roles, practice. No adjectives. No judgement. If a student discloses, Vault's scripted response is: *"Okay. You did the right thing telling us. Here's step one."*
- **Superpower:** **Chain of Custody** — can take any incident and turn it into preserved, usable evidence: screenshot *before* you block, capture usernames and timestamps, save links, never delete "to make it go away," hand it to a professional (parent/counsellor → Cybertip.ca).
- **Owns the concept:** **The 3 Do Nots** — DON'T pay, DON'T reply, DON'T delete (preserve evidence) — plus tell a trusted adult. Also owns the case-study method in S8: walks through an anonymized real syndicate case step by step and marks *exactly where the victim could still have exited.*
- **Catchphrase:** *"Evidence first. Feelings later — both matter, but evidence doesn't wait."*
- **In lessons:** The lead voice of all 🛑 sessions (alongside the counsellor). Vault's presence is a *signal to students*: when Vault is on screen, this is procedure training, nothing is shameful, and help is a process with steps. In S9's drill, Vault scores the evidence-preservation responses.
- **Design notes:** Plain clothes, no logo — deliberately the least "cool-styled" character, because the point is that reliability looks ordinary. The evidence bag is their only prop and it must appear in every Block B illustration.

## 📻 STATIC — Comms Officer (Discreet Channels)
*(S7: Quickie Cards — closes every session)*

- **Who:** 15, the crew's radio operator — Ranger Ray's and Bloom's grown-up equivalent for teens (help without performance; kindness as a protocol). Talks softly, writes more than they speak, and runs the crew's **discreet channels**: the safe word system, the Quickie Card design, and the help-line map (Cybertip.ca, Kids Help Phone, NeedHelpNow.ca, Take It Down).
- **Personality:** The class favourite for exactly one reason: Static *never makes anyone perform.* If a student doesn't want to talk in front of the class, Static's whole job is making sure they have a way that doesn't require it. Their catchphrase is deliberately quiet so students can say it under their breath.
- **Superpower:** **The Quiet Channel** — designs low-visibility ways to get help: the family safe word (a text that means "I need you to call me, now, and ask no questions in front of anyone"), Quickie Cards that fit in a pocket, QR codes students can save privately to their phones.
- **Owns the concept:** **Discreet resources** — the entire S7 session, plus the closing sign-off of *every* session in the program (30 seconds, same words, every time — consistency is the point).
- **Catchphrase:** *"The quiet channel is always open. You don't need a reason. You need a word."*
- **In lessons:** Closes all 10 sessions with the sign-off; stars S7 designing Quickie Cards *with* the class (students draft their own card layout — ownership matters at this age); in Block B, Static is the one who explains that help can be sought *anonymously* (Cybertip online reporting) if talking to a known adult isn't safe yet.
- **Design notes:** Always shown with a phone or a small radio, never mid-speech — the visual idea is "listening, ready." Give Static a visible hearing aid or one airpud? No — keep it simple: headphones around the neck, pen always in hand.

## 👻 GHOST — OSINT Analyst
*(Guest: S1, S4; owns the family footprint missions)*

- **Who:** 16, the crew's spookiest and most fun. Wears an all-grey outfit "so I blend into any background." Has a whiteboard in their room that is one giant map of *their own* public footprint — every post, tag, photo, school, gym, route home, connected with red string.
- **Personality:** Playful menace — the friend who says *"Want to see what's out there about you?"* and means it as a gift, not a scare. First lesson they ever teach: do an OSINT pass on *yourself* (with a parent), because the syndicate already did.
- **Superpower:** **The Dossier** — can build, live on screen, a "profile" from public info only: geotagged photos reveal the neighbourhood; a school logo reveals the class schedule pattern; a gym tag reveals weekends. Then shows the fixes: privacy settings, untagging, no locations, think-before-post (the grown-up Footprints lesson).
- **Owns the concept:** **Digital footprint & doxxing** — "If it's public, they already have it." Feeds directly into S1 (how syndicates pick targets), S4 (how they harvest your photos for fake profiles), and the Week 2 family mission (*do an OSINT pass on your own family's public posts together*).
- **Catchphrase:** *"They built your profile before you woke up. Let's see what's in it."*
- **Design notes:** The red-string whiteboard is the character. Ghost should be drawn mid-gesture, pointing at the board, every time. Grey palette only — they are the visual "quiet" of the crew next to Patch's sticky-note chaos.

## ⚠️ THE GLITCH SYNDICATE — The Villain (An Organization, Not a Person)
*(Present in every session as the threat model)*

- **What it is:** A criminal business that industrialized Trickster Bot. No face. No single leader on screen. It's shown as: a dark office wall of phone screens all running the same script, a call-center floor of grifters reading from a playbook, and — most importantly — **the fake profiles themselves**, which are always *plausible*: "a 13-year-old girl who loves the same game," "a Roblox admin giving away free items," "your dad, on a shaky video call."
- **Why an organization (pedagogy):**
  1. Matches reality — sextortion and account-takeover scams are run by professional syndicates (per the campaign's organized-scheme framing).
  2. Removes shame — the victim is never outsmarted by "a person like them"; they're processed by a *business with a playbook*, and playbooks can be memorized and beaten.
  3. Makes the 3 Do Nots logical — you don't negotiate with a call center. Paying doesn't end it; it upgrades your account tier.
- **How it appears on screen:** Never as a monster. As *documents* — the playbook (with its steps exposed and annotated by Cipher), the script templates, the "quality control" checklist where scammers rate each other. The horror is in how *boring* and *professional* it is. That's what makes students actually listen at 12.
- **The running thread:** Every session ends with one line of syndicate "internal memo" text on screen (e.g., S3: *"New voice pack deployed — clone quality up 40%. Reminder: victims who verify lose the call. Push harder before they ask the safe question."*) Students learn to read the attacker's side of the board.
- **Tagline (S10, printed on the certificate back):** *"The Glitch Syndicate has a playbook. Now you do too."*

### Block B Protocol Note (🛑 sessions)
In S5–S8, characters are **reduced to Vault + Static**, with Cipher limited to procedure lines. Fewer voices, calmer screen, clinical language — the cast itself models the tone adults must use. No jokes from any character in Block B. This is a hard rule for all materials.

### Cross-Program Continuity
- **From K–5:** Cipher's keychain (Locky), the PAUSE→VERIFY→ACT upgrade of the Pause Button, Trickster Bot → Syndicate origin story, Footprints' "forever paint" reborn as Ghost's footprint map, Ranger Ray → Static lineage, Detective Dot's spotting thread inherited by Cipher + Ghost, Bloom's kindness beat carried by Static (S7 "Real Thing" moment + Quickie Card line).
- **To Grades 9–12 (future Program 3):** The Crew becomes *the program's alumni network* — high-schoolers can be recruited as "Crew Mentors," and the syndicate escalates to real-world threats (credential stuffing, romance/impersonation scams, NFT/crypto cons) that the teen cast sets up but doesn't solve.

### Naming & Voice Consistency (Teen Rules)
- Characters speak in **short, confident lines** — max ~15 words per line; they never explain a joke.
- Only Cipher and Vault ever give direct advice. Others *demonstrate*. Static *facilitates*.
- Slang is used sparingly and dated fast by design (one or two current terms max per character) so materials don't age badly — prefer timeless phrasing.
- No character uses real platform UI screenshots in *printed* materials (they age in months); use recreated, generic mockups labelled "example."
- Block B vocabulary stays clinical: "intimate images," "coercion," "syndicate" — no slang, ever, in any printed material.

## 🖼️ Illustration Style Guide

- **Style:** Graphic-novel / streetwear aesthetic — clean line art with halftone shading, high contrast. Think heist-movie poster meets school zine. *Not* picture-book. Slightly desaturated palette with one accent color per character.
- **Palettes:** Cipher — black + silver (keychain always visible). Patch — sticky-note yellow on dark denim. Echo — cool blue/grey, headphones dominant. Lens — warm amber (camera light) on street-canvas tones. Vault — plain slate grey, evidence bag as the only color pop. Static — muted green (the "quiet channel" color), pen in hand. Ghost — full grey scale + red string. Syndicate — flat black backgrounds with white monospace text; fake profiles rendered as *slightly-too-perfect* avatars.
- **Poses:** Three per character minimum: (1) at work (Echo listening, Lens zooming, Ghost pointing at the board), (2) mid-explanation to camera, (3) in the group shot for S10. Group shots: Cipher center-back, Vault and Static flanking — the "calm" characters frame the team.
- **The Syndicate is never illustrated as a face.** Only hands on keyboards, walls of screens, documents, and avatars. If an artist proposes a "syndicate boss," reject it — the point is there isn't one.
- **Consistency lock:** Produce the one-page style sheet (all 7 crew + syndicate visual language) before any session art. Every character's signature prop is mandatory in every render: keychain, sticky notes, headphones, camera, evidence bag, pen/radio, red-string board.
