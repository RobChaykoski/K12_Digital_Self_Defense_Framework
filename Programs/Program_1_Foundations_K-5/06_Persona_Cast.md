# 🦸 Persona Cast: "The Cyber Squad" (K–Grade 5)

**Program:** Digital Foundations | **Ages:** 5–11 | **Tracks:** Little Guardians (K–2) & Digital Detectives (3–5)

---

## 💡 Design Philosophy

Every persona is built from **real cyber security culture**, translated into kid language:

| Cyber Security Culture | Kid Translation |
|------------------------|-----------------|
| The firewall / shield | A literal glowing shield character |
| Passwords & encryption locks | A locksmith with a key that only *they* trust others with |
| Phishing detection ("spot the fake") | A detective who sniffs out disguises |
| AI & deepfakes ("synthetic media forensics") | A scanner that can tell real from robot-made |
| Digital footprint / privacy | A painter whose footprints dry forever |
| Reporting incidents (Cybertip.ca, trusted adult) | A radio operator — the "always-on" helper |
| Kindness & anti-bullying (digital citizenship) | The squad's heart, not just its shield |
| Scam syndicates (the villain, made safe) | A cartoon trickster who *loses* every time a kid reports him |

**Rules for all personas:**
1. **No shaming.** Characters make mistakes and fix them — that's the whole point.
2. **Diverse by default.** The squad is intentionally mixed in gender, culture, ability (one character uses a wheelchair), and species (animals + humans).
3. **One concept per hero.** Each persona owns exactly one safety skill so kids can say *"that's [Name]'s rule!"*
4. **The villain never wins on screen.** Trickster Bot is silly, not scary — he loses whenever a kid tells an adult.

## 🦊 CAPTAIN SHIELD — Team Leader
*(Both tracks)*

- **Who:** A friendly, calm kid in a navy hoodie with a glowing silver shield emblem. Carries a small tablet called the **"Squad Screen."**
- **Personality:** Warm, patient, never laughs at mistakes. First words after any problem: *"Good news — you can fix this. Let's do it together."*
- **Superpower:** **The Pause Button.** Can freeze time for 10 seconds so anyone can think before they click, type, or share.
- **Owns the concept:** The core instinct behind every lesson — *Stop → Think → Act.* This is Track A's "green vs. red button" skill and Track B's "ask before you click."
- **Catchphrase:** *"Pause. Think. You've got this."*
- **In lessons:** Opens and closes every session; appears on the title slide of each lesson plan and on the certificate at graduation. Kids press their own "pause button" (a printed paper shield) when they want to signal *stop, I need a grown-up.*
- **Design notes for illustrator:** Hoodie with subtle circuit-board pattern in the fabric; shield glows soft blue; approachable face, no teeth-baring hero pose — Captain Shield is always shown sitting or kneeling at kid height.

## 🔐 LOCKY THE FOX — The Locksmith
*(Track A: K–2, primary)*

- **Who:** A small orange fox in a tool belt full of keys, wearing round glasses. Carries one special key on a red ribbon: the **"Trusted Adult Key."**
- **Personality:** Secretive by *choice* — treats every key like a treasure. Loves lock-and-key games and "guess my combo" puzzles.
- **Superpower:** Can see invisible locks around everything: doors, diaries, phones… and around **passwords.** Shows kids the lock around their own password and demonstrates that only one key (the trusted adult) should ever be shared.
- **Owns the concept:** Passwords = locks. *Your password is your key — you give it to a trusted grown-up, and NO ONE else, not even an online "friend."* Directly supports Track A Session 2 (*The Password Lock*) and Track B Session 3 (*Password Power*, where Locky appears as a guest).
- **Catchphrase:** *"Keys stay in the family. Always."*
- **In lessons:** Puppet or cutout for the lock game; stars the "Password Scavenger Hunt" where kids find picture-hints that make strong passphrase words (Track B).
- **Design notes:** Fox, not a human — younger kids project onto animals more easily. Tool belt has exactly 4 keys (easy to count in circle time). The red ribbon key is the visual anchor for "trusted adult."

## 🔍 DETECTIVE DOT — The Spotter
*(Track B: Grades 3–5, primary)*

- **Who:** A sharp-eyed kid detective with a magnifying glass that has a tiny screen inside it (shows warning signs as icons). Wears a yellow trench coat and carries the **"Fake-or-Real Folder."**
- **Personality:** Curious, asks *too many* questions ("Wait — who is this email FROM? What did they say I WON?"). Has a habit of circling suspicious words in red.
- **Superpower:** **The Sniffer.** Her magnifying glass glows when something is fake: free prizes from strangers, "click here NOW!!" links, emails asking for passwords or card numbers.
- **Owns the concept:** Phishing detection — the **3 Warning Signs** (1. Too good to be true, 2. Hurry hurry hurry, 3. Asks for your secrets). Stars Track B Sessions 1 (*Real vs. Fake Email*), 5 (*Too Good to Be True*) and 7 (*Phishing Scavenger Hunt*).
- **Catchphrase:** *"If it smells fishy online… it's probably a fake."* (Yes, the pun is intentional — it's the one kids remember forever.)
- **In lessons:** Runs the phishing scavenger hunt as the "case file" narrator; her folder contains the 12 practice scam messages. In Track A she appears only briefly as the "big-kid detective" to seed aspiration.
- **Design notes:** The magnifying glass is her logo — it doubles as a printable icon for worksheets (circle the fishy stuff *like Detective Dot*).
- **Successor (Grades 6–8):** Dot's spotting thread doesn't end — it gets staff. **Cipher** inherits the method (her 3 Warning Signs become the crew's PAUSE → VERIFY → ACT protocol) and **Ghost** inherits the investigation (the magnifying glass becomes the OSINT dossier). Her magnifying-glass icon reappears on the Program 2 verification checklist so returning students see their own childhood tool in the new kit.

## 🤖 PIXEL — The AI Scanner
*(Track B: Grades 3–5, primary)*

- **Who:** A small, round robot with one big friendly eye that works like a scanner. Wears an oversized hoodie and has a "BFF" human friend (a kid named Sam) who always carries Pixel around on their shoulder.
- **Personality:** Honest to a fault — *literally cannot lie or pretend.* When kids ask if he's real, he says: *"I'm made by humans with code! And that means other things can be made too. Let me show you."*
- **Superpower:** **The Beep.** Pixel scans any picture, voice, or video and beeps GREEN (real) or PURPLE (might be robot-made). When purple, he explains *how* to check: weird edges, voices that sound "off," asking the person a question only the real them would know.
- **Owns the concept:** AI basics & deepfakes — *"A computer can copy what it's seen."* Stars Track B Session 4 (*What is AI?*) and supports the deepfake hook in the lesson plans (clearly-labelled fake clip of a cartoon character).
- **Catchphrase:** *"Beep! Let me check if that's real… or robot-real."*
- **In lessons:** The "robot" in the Real-or-Robot game — kids sort printed images; Pixel's card flips to confirm. Also appears in Track A as a story character ("What is this small robot?") without teaching anything yet — just planting the idea that *not everything online is what it seems.*
- **Design notes:** Round, soft, no sharp parts — must read as a buddy, never a threat. Purple = "check it" color (avoids red so it doesn't feel like danger). Sam should be drawn with a distinct skin tone and hair to model diversity in the "human + machine" pairing.

## 🐼 FOOTPRINTS THE PANDA — The Painter
*(Both tracks, primary K–2)*

- **Who:** A panda who walks through a magical paint trail — every step leaves a paw print that **dries and stays forever**, no matter how much water you throw at it.
- **Personality:** Careful and a little dramatic ("Oh no! I painted my name on the whole playground!"). Loves cleaning things up — but learns some paint can't be cleaned.
- **Superpower:** **The Forever Paint.** Can show what happens when something is posted online: you can take the brush away, but the paint stays. Also shows *kind* footprints (praise, help) vs. *graffiti* footprints (mean words) — both last forever.
- **Owns the concept:** Digital footprints & privacy — *"What you post can stay forever."* Stars Track A Session 5 (*Digital Footprints*) and appears in Track B for the "what would future-you want painted?" reflection (university, jobs, friends).
- **Catchphrase:** *"Think before you step! That paint never dries away… it just dries."*
- **In lessons:** The classroom floor game — actual painted footprints on butcher paper that kids walk across as "posts"; nothing gets erased. Track B extends it: the class builds a "footprint wall" of things they'd be proud to have stay.
- **Design notes:** Panda chosen because black-and-white paw prints read instantly as "prints." The paint is always soft colors (blue/green) — never red, which is reserved for Trickster Bot.

## 📡 RANGER RAY — The Radio Operator
*(Both tracks, primary K–2)*

- **Who:** A kid in a forest-green ranger vest with a walkie-talkie that is **always on and always answered.** Uses a wheelchair (drawn confidently, as the coolest mobility in the squad).
- **Personality:** Calm under pressure. The one who says *"That's not your fault, and you did exactly right by telling me."* Never surprised, never mad — only ready to help.
- **Superpower:** **The Open Channel.** Can connect any kid, anywhere, instantly to a trusted adult or a real help line. In stories, his radio literally glows when someone needs to say "this feels weird."
- **Owns the concept:** **Tell a trusted adult — and it's SAFE to tell.** No device confiscation, no shame. For Track B, Ray is also the bridge to real reporting: **Cybertip.ca** (report), **Kids Help Phone** (talk to someone). This is the single most important persona in the program: every other skill fails if a kid won't speak up.
- **Catchphrase:** *"Your radio is always on. Say the word 'weird' and I'm coming."*
- **In lessons:** Every session ends with Ray's sign-off (30 seconds, scripted). The walkie-talkie becomes a classroom prop — when a kid in role-play needs help, they "talk to Ray." Track B adds: kids write their own *personal safe word* for the family.
- **Design notes:** The wheelchair is non-negotiable — it normalizes ability diversity without ever being "the disability character" (Ray's whole identity is *reliability*). Radio glow = soft green, the "safe" color used across all materials.

## 🌸 BLOOM — The Heart
*(Track B: Grades 3–5, primary)*

- **Who:** A creative kid with paint-stained fingers (a nod to Footprints) who runs a tiny zine called **"The Real Thing"** — where she posts what actually happens, not the highlight reel.
- **Personality:** Funny, warm, slightly sassy. The first to say *"Wait, that 'perfect' photo? She told me it took 40 tries and one spilled juice box."*
- **Superpower:** **The Un-Filter.** Can peel back any "perfect" online moment to show the messy, real one underneath — the retakes, the group chats where everyone's insecure too, the comments that get deleted.
- **Owns the concept:** The **highlight reel isn't real life** (Track B Session 2) + kindness/digital citizenship. Also owns "mean words travel as fast as kind ones" alongside Footprints' forever paint.
- **Catchphrase:** *"Behind every filter is a regular person. Probably one who spilled something today."*
- **In lessons:** Hosts the "Un-Filter" activity: pairs of cards — glossy "online version" vs. real-life version; kids match them and talk. Also leads the class pledge that closes Session 2.
- **Design notes:** Give Bloom a visible, specific cultural marker (e.g., hair in braids with colorful ties) so diversity is *designed in*, not an afterthought. Her zine pages are printable classroom art.
- **Successor (Grades 6–8):** Bloom's kindness theme lives on through **Static** — the safe word and the quiet channel are her Un-Filter grown up (helping someone without making them perform). The "Real Thing" beat returns in Program 2 Session 7 (a highlight-reel check-in) and as one line on every Quickie Card, so the theme never disappears for two years.

## ⚠️ TRICKSTER BOT — The (Silly) Villain
*(Both tracks — appears from Session 1)*

- **Who:** A glitchy, lopsided robot wearing a crooked cowboy hat and a too-big "WINNER!!" sash. Everything about him is slightly *off* — one ear bent, text on his chest misspelled ("FREE STUFF!!!").
- **Personality:** Desperately try-hard. His scams are obvious to adults but genuinely tempting to kids: *"FREE 1,000 COINS!! Just type your password!"* He monologues, trips over his own cables, and gets dramatically deflated when a kid reports him.
- **Owns the concept:** Personifies **every scam pattern** in one safe face — free-stuff traps (Track B S5), password fishing (S1/S3), fake friends online (Track A S1). Kids learn to *name* him: "Oh, that's Trickster Bot's trick."
- **The Rule of the Villain:** He **never** threatens, never looks scary, and **always loses** — because in every story his defeat is a kid telling an adult or not clicking. He ends each appearance deflating into a small pile of parts with a tiny sign: *"Reported. Thanks for playing."*
- **Catchphrase:** *"FREE FREE FREE!!! …(no wait, you have to give me your password first)"*
- **In lessons:** The "red" in every sorting game; the target of the phishing scavenger hunt (find all 12 of Trickster Bot's disguises); the character who gets "defeated" at both graduation ceremonies by the class chanting *"Pause. Think. Report."*
- **Design notes:** Must be *funny*, not frightening — think cartoon clown-bot, not horror. Misspellings on him are deliberate and double as teaching (kids spot the typos = first phishing skill, Track B).

## 🎭 How to Use the Cast (Quick Reference)

### Session Structure
| Moment | Who appears | Why |
|--------|-------------|-----|
| Opening (2 min) | Captain Shield + today's hero | Sets the one concept for the day |
| Story/hook | Today's hero + Trickster Bot (when relevant) | Conflict = the threat, in kid language |
| Activity | Hero as narrator/prop | Hands-on skill practice |
| Closing (1 min) | Ranger Ray | *"Radio's always on. Say 'weird' and I'm coming."* |

### Track A (K–2) Roster
Captain Shield (lead), Locky, Footprints, Ranger Ray + Trickster Bot. Pixel appears in stories only (no skills yet).

### Track B (3–5) Roster
Captain Shield (lead), Detective Dot, Pixel, Bloom, Ranger Ray + Trickster Bot. Locky and Footprints appear as guests in Sessions 3 and the footprint-wall activity.

### Cross-Grade "Detective Mentors" Program
Grades 3–5 mentor K–2 during crafts: each Track B student is assigned a hero to "channel" (Dot mentors the lock game, Pixel mentors the robot sort, etc.). Mentoring reinforces their own skills — and gives them a role model identity.

### Printables & Props Checklist
- [ ] Paper "Pause Button" shields for every desk (Captain Shield)
- [ ] Locky puppet or 12-inch cutout + 4 foam keys
- [ ] Detective Dot magnifying-glass icon for worksheets (printable, black/white OK)
- [ ] Pixel "beep card" — flips GREEN / PURPLE (laminated)
- [ ] Butcher-paper footprint trail + washable blue/green paint (Footprints)
- [ ] One walkie-talkie as the classroom "Ray radio" prop
- [ ] Trickster Bot poster with his 12 disguises (scavenger hunt target sheet)
- [ ] Graduation certificate: all 8 characters in a row, kid's name in the middle

### Naming & Voice Consistency
- Every character speaks in **short sentences** (K–2 material: max 8 words per line).
- Only Captain Shield and Ranger Ray ever give direct advice to the camera/reader. Heroes *demonstrate*; they don't lecture.
- Trickster Bot is always written with his misspellings — never "corrected" in print.
- No character mentions real brands (Roblox, Discord, etc.) — keep personas platform-neutral so materials last years.

## 🖼️ Illustration Style Guide (for artist or AI image generation)

- **Style:** Flat vector, rounded shapes, thick soft outlines — friendly picture-book meets modern app icon. Think *Bluey* warmth with a subtle tech sparkle in the backgrounds.
- **Palette:** Navy + silver (Shield), orange (Locky), yellow (Dot), purple/green (Pixel), blue-green paint (Footprints), forest green (Ray), coral/pink (Bloom), glitchy red-black with misspelled text (Trickster Bot).
- **Backgrounds:** Simple "digital frontier" scenes — pixelated prairies, glowing trails, a horizon where the old West meets the new web. Ties into the program's Wild West framing without guns or danger.
- **Poses:** Always at kid eye-level; heroes sitting, kneeling, or small-and-round. Trickster Bot is always slightly too big for his own hat/sash.
- **Consistency lock:** Generate/reference all 8 characters from one style sheet before any lesson art. Each character needs 3 poses minimum: standing with arms open (title slides), pointing at something (instruction moments), and deflated/celebrating (end of session).
