# 🛡️ Program 1: DIGITAL FOUNDATIONS (K–Grade 5)

**Ages:** 5–11 | **Duration:** One school term (6–8 weeks) | **Format:** In-class sessions + home reinforcement

---

## 📌 Program Overview

Program 1 consolidates the two early-phase plans into a single K–Grade 5 curriculum with two tracks:

| Track | Grades | Sessions | Length | Focus |
|-------|--------|----------|--------|-------|
| **Track A: Little Guardians** | K–2 | 6 sessions | 30 min each | Core instincts — stranger danger, password locks, green/red buttons, screen time, digital footprints |
| **Track B: Digital Detectives** | 3–5 | 8 sessions | 40 min each | First real threats — phishing emails, social media traps, passphrases, AI basics, scam spotting, safe clicking |

**Design principle:** Younger learners build *instincts and vocabulary*; older learners build *recognition skills and habits*. Both tracks end with a celebration/certification session to motivate families.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

### Track A (K–2)
By the end of Program 1, students will be able to:
1. Explain that people online may not be who they say they are ("stranger danger online")
2. Say their password only to a trusted adult — never to a "friend" online
3. Distinguish safe (green) actions from risky (red) actions when prompted online
4. Accept screen-time boundaries as fair rules, like other family rules
5. Understand that things posted online can stay forever ("digital footprints")
6. Tell a trusted adult about anything online that feels weird or scary — without fear of losing their device

### Track B (3–5)
By the end of Program 1, students will be able to:
1. Identify at least 3 warning signs of a phishing/fake email or message
2. Explain why social media shows a "highlight reel" that isn't real life
3. Build and store a strong passphrase (not just a password)
4. Describe in simple terms what AI is and how it can make fake images, voices, and text
5. Spot "too good to be true" online offers (free games, prizes, coins)
6. Practice link-hovering / "ask before you click" habits
7. Complete a phishing scavenger hunt identifying real scam patterns
8. Apply all skills in a mock scenario without adult prompting

## 🧭 Pedagogical Approach

1. **Story-first (K–2):** Every lesson opens with a short story or puppet scenario (sample: "The Internet Playground," in the Lesson Plans file)
2. **Concrete metaphors:** passwords = locks, footprints = paint that dries, AI = a computer that copies things it has seen
3. **No shaming:** The message is always *"It's okay to make mistakes — tell an adult and we fix it."* Devices are never taken away as punishment for telling the truth
4. **Practice over lecture (3–5):** minimum 15 min of hands-on activity per session (worksheets, sorting cards, computer lab)
5. **Family loop:** every session sends a one-page "Home Mission" so parents can reinforce the exact same vocabulary at home

## 🚨 Teacher Tips (Both Tracks)
- **Language:** Use "a trusted adult" instead of "your parents" — some students live with grandparents, foster families, or have complicated home situations
- **Special needs:** All games work in low-tech mode (paper cards instead of devices); provide picture supports for ELL and SPED students; keep K–2 sessions movement-friendly
- **If a student discloses something** (e.g., someone asked them to send a photo): stay calm, thank them for telling, do NOT interrogate, and follow your school's disclosure protocol. Contact the guidance counsellor and, if appropriate, report to **Cybertip.ca**. For youth support: **NeedHelpNow.ca**
- **Parent communication:** Send the Parent Toolkit intro page home before Week 1 so families know what vocabulary is coming
