# 👪 Program 1 — Parent & Guardian Toolkit (K–Grade 5)

*Send the intro page home before Week 1; send Home Missions with each session.*

---

## 📣 Intro Page: What Your Child Will Learn This Term

This term, your child joins **Digital Foundations** — a K–Grade 5 program that builds good online instincts before bad habits form. They'll learn our shared vocabulary:

- **"Anyone could be anyone"** — people online may not be who they say
- **Keys only to family** — passwords are never shared, even with "friends"
- **Green vs. Red** — safe actions vs. actions that need an adult first
- **Digital footprints** — things posted can stay forever
- **Highlight reels** — social media shows the best moments, not real life
- **The 3 Clues of a Fake** — rush/fear, asks for info or clicks, sender looks off
- **Passphrases** — three random words beat one weak password
- **AI can fake it** — images, voices, and text can all be made by machines

### The One Rule That Matters Most
**If anything online feels weird, scary, or "too good," they tell you — and the device is NOT taken away as punishment.** Children who fear losing their tablet stay silent. Children who are safe to speak up protect themselves.

## 🏠 Home Missions (one per session — 5 minutes each)

### Track A (K–2)
| Session | Mission |
|---|---|
| 1 | Play "Mask Game" at dinner: wear a hat, say "I'm your teacher!" Ask how they'd know it's not you. Online, anyone could be anyone. |
| 2 | Ask them to show you their padlock. Quiz: "A friend online asks for your password — what do you say?" |
| 3 | At mealtimes or in the car, read 4 quick scenarios; they shout GREEN or RED. |
| 4 | Set ONE screen-time boundary together this week and post it where the device lives. Praise them for following it. |
| 5 | Ask: "If you posted a drawing today, would you still like it in 10 years?" Talk about one thing they'd never post (address, school, photo with strangers). |
| 6 | **Family Pledge night:** sign the pledge below together; display their cape. |

### Track B (3–5)
| Session | Mission |
|---|---|
| 1 | Show them a real phishing email you've received (blacked out) and ask them to find the clues. Or use Handout B1 at the kitchen table. |
| 2 | Watch one influencer post together. Ask: "Reel or Real? What's missing from this picture of their life?" |
| 3 | Have them build a passphrase with you (3 random words + number/symbol). Change ONE family account to it if appropriate. Never write it on the box! |
| 4 | Show them an AI image (or generate one together, clearly labelled). Ask: "What gives it away?" Practice the tell list. |
| 5 | Hunt for one real scam ad together (search "free [game] codes"). Point out every trick on the checklist. |
| 6 | Next time they're about to tap a link from a message, pause together: hover/read first. Make it a family habit. |
| 7 | Play the scavenger hunt at home: hide 3 note cards (2 real, 1 scam) around the house. |
| 8 | **Family Challenge:** each family member finds one "Reel or Real" example this week and shares it at dinner. Frame their certificate! |

## 🤝 The Family Pledge (Session 6 / 8)
> In our family:
> - We tell each other about anything online that feels weird, scary, or too good to be true — **without anger and without losing the device**.
> - Passwords stay in the family. Kids' accounts are managed by a grown-up.
> - Before posting, we ask: *will I still be okay with this in 10 years?*
> - We keep screens off during meals and out of bedrooms at night.
> - If something really bad happens (someone asks for photos, threatens, or pressures), we go straight to a trusted adult and can report it to **Cybertip.ca** (confidential, free).

## 🗣️ Key Takeaways for Families
- **Privacy is the foundation:** nothing personal (name, school, address, photos of home) goes online without a grown-up checking. For young children, keep profiles private and manage accounts yourself.
- **Screens are normal — boundaries make them healthy.** Consistency matters more than the exact number of minutes.
- **Kids DO see bad things online** (violence, adult content, scam ads). If it happens: stay calm, don't interrogate, talk about what they saw, and adjust filters. You can also call **NeedHelpNow.ca** for youth support guidance.
- **Filters are a net, not a wall.** They reduce risk but never replace the conversation — the goal is a child who comes to you.
- **If someone asks your child for photos, money, or personal details:** don't click or reply, screenshot everything, tell the child they did right by coming to you, and report at **Cybertip.ca** or to police. For image-related incidents, **Take It Down** (NCMEC) offers anonymous removal help.

## 📞 If Something Happens — Who to Call (Canada)
| Resource | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Cybertip.ca** | Canada's national tipline — report cybercrime incl. scams, threats, and any request for photos from a child. Confidential. |
| **NeedHelpNow.ca** | Support for youth dealing with online harm, image sharing, cyberbullying. |
| **Take It Down (NCMEC)** | Anonymous takedowns of explicit images of minors. |
| **911 / local police** | Immediate danger, threats of violence, or a child in distress. |

## 🧰 Suggested Home Setup (K–5)
- [ ] Content filters + safe search ON for all family devices (router-level is best)
- [ ] Kids' accounts created and managed by a parent; privacy set to the strictest option
- [ ] Devices charge in a common area at night — no phones in bedrooms
- [ ] A visible "tell an adult" rule posted near where devices are used
- [ ] This toolkit's contact page saved in the family phone
