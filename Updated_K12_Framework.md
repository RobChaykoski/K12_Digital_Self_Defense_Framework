# K-12 Digital Self-Defense & Cyber Safety Education Framework
## "The Wild West Online: Navigating New & Old Threats"

**Prepared for:** Local School Board / Community Education Initiative  
**Based on:** Digital Self-Defense & Cyber Safety Month Campaign (October 2026)  
**Target Audience:** Kindergarten through Grade 12 (K-12)  

---

## 🗺️ Framework Overview

This progressive education framework takes students from foundational digital literacy in Kindergarten through advanced threat detection in Grade 12. It covers both **legacy threats** (phishing, social media traps) and **emerging threats** (AI/Deepfakes, Sextortion, Crypto Scams) in age-appropriate ways.

The "Wild West" metaphor helps students understand that the internet is a place of opportunity but also requires protection, awareness, and smart decision-making — much like frontier towns needed both cowboys and sheriffs.

## 📚 PHASE 1: Elementary (K-5)
### Theme: "My Digital Body & Online Space"

#### Kindergarten - Grade 2 (Ages 5-8)
**Goal:** Build foundational awareness of online safety through storytelling and routine habits.

| Topic | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Who am I talking to?** | Identify familiar vs. unfamiliar people online | "Stranger Danger" digital version with cartoon characters |
| **The Password Lock** | Understand passwords as keys to your door | Create a physical "password lock" craft; practice saying passwords out loud (using fictional examples only) |
| **Clicking Safely** | Distinguish safe links vs. suspicious links | Game: "Tap the Green Button, Not the Red One" |
| **Screen Time Boundaries** | Recognize healthy tech habits | Family screen-time contract; visual timer activities |
| **Digital Footprint** | Understand that online actions stay forever | "Footprints in the Sand" analogy — what disappears vs. what stays |

**Key Takeaway:** *The internet is like a playground — you can have fun, but always know who's watching and where your gate is.*

#### Grades 3-5 (Ages 8-11)
**Goal:** Introduce basic phishing, social media basics, and early AI awareness.

| Topic | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Phishing Basics** | Spot a fake email or message | "Real vs. Fake" worksheet with school newsletter examples |
| **Social Media Traps** | Understand likes, comments, and comparison | "The Highlight Reel" — social media shows only the best moments |
| **Password Power** | Create stronger passwords | Passphrase building exercise (e.g., "BlueTiger$RunsFast!") |
| **Intro to AI** | What is a computer making things? | Compare AI-generated art vs. human-made art; discuss prompts |
| **The "Too Good to Be True" Rule** | Identify basic scams | Story-based learning: free Robux, free Fortnite skins, etc. |

**Key Takeaway:** *Not everything online is real — and that's okay if you know how to check.*

## 📚 PHASE 2: Middle School (Grades 6-8)
### Theme: "Own Your Space: Privacy, AI & Digital Self-Defense"

#### Grades 6-8 (Ages 11-14)
**Goal:** Develop deeper understanding of online identity, social engineering, and digital resilience.

| Topic | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Credential Reuse Risks** | Understand why using the same password everywhere is dangerous | "Password Chain" simulation game |
| **Fake Login Pages** | Recognize signs of fake login pages | Create a fake login page and then identify the differences |
| **Social Engineering Tactics** | Identify common manipulation techniques | Role-play scenarios with urgency, authority, fear, scarcity, curiosity, and reward |
| **Password Managers** | Understand how to use a password manager | Demo password manager with fictional accounts |
| **Phishing-Resistant Authentication** | Learn about passkeys and phishing-resistant authentication | Introduction to WebAuthn/FIDO2 concepts |
| **Voice Cloning & Deepfakes** | Understand the risks of synthetic media | Analyze a deepfake video with a focus on source verification |
| **Sextortion Awareness** | Recognize warning signs and know how to respond | Review Canadian support resources and response protocols |
| **Reporting & Help-Seeking** | Know how to report incidents and ask for help | Practice using reporting tools |

**Key Takeaway:** *You're not alone — if something feels wrong, tell a trusted adult.*

---

## 📚 PHASE 3: High School (Grades 9-12)
### Theme: "Digital Independence: Managing Risk & Protecting Your Future"

#### Grades 9-10 (Ages 14-16)
**Goal:** Introduce personal cybersecurity planning, information integrity, and legal awareness.

| Topic | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Account Security Assessment** | Evaluate the strength of online accounts | Practice with fictional persona accounts |
| **Authentication Strength** | Compare different authentication methods | Evaluate SMS, TOTP, passkeys, and security keys |
| **Phishing-Resistant Authentication** | Understand passkeys and security keys | Hands-on introduction to phishing-resistant authentication |
| **Recovery Channel Protection** | Secure account recovery information | Practice protecting recovery codes |
| **Device Encryption** | Understand encryption concepts | Explore how encryption protects data |
| **Least Privilege Principle** | Learn about access control | Apply concepts to app permissions |
| **Financial Scams** | Identify common financial fraud patterns | Analyze real-world examples |
| **Canadian Legal Awareness** | Understand legal responsibilities | Review Criminal Code s.162.1 for intimate image publication |
| **Media Provenance** | Evaluate the source and authenticity of digital content | Practice source tracing and corroboration |

**Key Takeaway:** *Your digital life is built on choices — make them wisely.*

#### Grades 11-12 (Ages 16-18)
**Goal:** Develop advanced cybersecurity thinking and ethical decision-making.

| Topic | Learning Objective | Activity Example |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Threat Modeling** | Assess digital risks and vulnerabilities | Apply threat modeling to personal and organizational scenarios |
| **Defense in Depth** | Understand layered security concepts | Design a security plan with multiple controls |
| **Access Control** | Learn about permissions and data protection | Evaluate application and network permissions |
| **Data States** | Understand data at rest, in transit, and in use | Explore how data is protected in different states |
| **Cloud and IoT Risks** | Identify risks associated with connected devices | Analyze security risks of smart home devices |
| **Backup and Recovery** | Plan for data protection and recovery | Develop a personal recovery strategy |
| **Incident Response** | Learn how to respond to security incidents | Participate in a tabletop exercise |
| **Ethical Cybersecurity** | Understand professional ethics and boundaries | Discuss responsible disclosure and acceptable use |
| **Algorithmic Influence** | Understand how algorithms shape information | Analyze algorithmic bias and influence |

**Key Takeaway:** *You are your own digital sheriff — protect your future.*

## 📚 Core Cyber Hygiene Topics (Integrated Across All Grades)

All students should receive instruction on these essential cybersecurity topics throughout their K-12 education:

- Software Updates: Understanding the importance of keeping devices and applications up-to-date
- Device Locking and Loss: How to secure devices and respond to loss
- Safe Downloads: Identifying safe sources and avoiding malware
- Application Permissions: Understanding what apps can access
- Public/Shared Networks: Recognizing risks on public Wi-Fi
- Backups: Creating and maintaining backups of important data
- Malware: Identifying and avoiding malicious software
- Account Recovery: Understanding and protecting recovery mechanisms
- Data Protection: Safeguarding personal information
- Incident Recovery: Responding to and recovering from security incidents

## 📚 Canadian Legal Framework

### Publication of Intimate Images without Consent
The publication or threatened publication of intimate images without consent is addressed in Criminal Code s.162.1, not s.276. This section also expressly covers certain AI-generated depictions and threats to publish.

### Sextortion Awareness
Students should understand that:
- Paying demands does not guarantee threats will stop and may lead to further demands
- Students should stop communication, retain screenshots, never pay or send more content, block, seek help, and report
- Support resources include Cybertip.ca and other Canadian victim support organizations

## 📚 Authentication Guidance

### Modern Best Practices
While TOTP MFA is useful, neither TOTP nor SMS is phishing-resistant. Passkeys/FIDO2 and phishing-resistant authentication should be introduced progressively from Grades 6-8 and practiced in Grades 9-12.

### Phishing-Resistant Authentication
- **Passkeys/FIDO2**: These are phishing-resistant and bind authentication to the legitimate domain
- **Security Keys**: Physical devices that provide strong authentication
- **Hardware Tokens**: Devices that generate one-time codes without SMS dependency
- **SMS and TOTP**: While better than no authentication, these remain vulnerable to phishing attacks

## 📚 Deepfake and Misinformation Instruction

### Modern Approach
The framework now emphasizes:
- Source tracing and reverse search
- Corroboration of information
- Provenance and confidence statements
- Independent identity verification
- Visual artifacts as supporting clues, not definitive proof

### Replacing Visual "Tells"
Instead of focusing on visual cues like "fingers" or "lip-sync" which are unreliable, students learn to:
- Use a known callback channel
- Introduce delay before acting
- Verify identity through independent means
- Seek corroboration from multiple sources

## 📚 Safety Guidelines

### Practice Without Personal Exposure
Classroom exercises use:
- Fictional personas
- Demo accounts
- Synthetic messages
- Sandbox environments
- Never real passwords, credentials, or personal information

### Safeguarding Protocols
For sensitive topics like sextortion:
- Counselor presence during lessons
- Clear response protocols (stop, save evidence, report, tell a safe adult)
- Mandatory reporting procedures
- Student opt-out options
- Crisis referral mechanisms

## 📚 Curriculum Progression

### Developmentally Appropriate Autonomy
- **K-2**: STOP → ASK AN ADULT
- **Grades 3-5**: PAUSE → CHECK → ASK → REPORT
- **Grades 6-8**: PAUSE → VERIFY → PROTECT → REPORT
- **Grades 9-10**: ASSESS → VERIFY → PROTECT → RECOVER
- **Grades 11-12**: ASSESS RISK → SELECT CONTROLS → RESPOND → RECOVER → IMPROVE

## 📚 Framework Architecture

The framework has been reorganized into four developmental stages:

### Stage 1: Digital Safety Foundations (K-2)
**Goal:** Build foundational awareness of online safety
**Exit Competency:** I know when to stop and ask for help.

### Stage 2: Digital Self-Defense Foundations (Grades 3-5)
**Goal:** Introduce basic cybersecurity concepts and risk recognition
**Exit Competency:** I can recognize common risks and protect my information.

### Stage 3: Digital Resilience (Grades 6-8)
**Goal:** Develop skills in authentication, verification, and incident response
**Exit Competency:** I can recognize manipulation, verify requests, protect accounts, and respond safely.

### Stage 4: Cybersecurity & Digital Independence (Grades 9-12)
**Goal:** Develop advanced cybersecurity thinking and independent digital decision-making
**Exit Competency:** I can assess digital risk, select appropriate protections, verify information, respond to incidents, and make responsible independent decisions.

## 📚 Assessment and Evaluation

### Transfer-Based Assessment
Assessments should measure transfer, not disclosure or personal configuration:
- Sandbox demonstrations
- Scenario explanations
- Decision trees
- Teach-backs
- Anonymized portfolios

### Mastery Rubrics
Each grade band should include measurable mastery outcomes with clear rubrics.
