# Phase 1 Plan: Kindergarten – Grade 2 (Ages 5–8)
## "My Digital Body & Online Space"

> *The internet is like a playground — you can have fun, but always know who's watching and where your gate is.*

---

### 📋 Plan Overview

| Detail | Information |
|--------|-------------|
| **Duration** | 6 sessions × 30 minutes (or 2 × 45-min blocks) |
| **Delivery** | Classroom-based with teacher facilitation |
| **Materials Needed** | Projector, printed worksheets, craft supplies, tablet/computer for demos |
| **Prerequisites** | Basic familiarity with tablets or classroom devices |
| **Cross-Curricular Links** | Health (screen time), Art (digital drawing), Language Arts (storytelling) |

---

### 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this phase, students will be able to:
1. Identify familiar vs. unfamiliar people in online games and video calls
2. Explain what a password is and why it's like a door lock
3. Distinguish between safe ("green") and unsafe ("red") links/buttons on screen
4. Understand that their digital actions leave "footprints" that stay forever
5. Recognize healthy vs. unhealthy screen time habits

---

### 📅 Session Breakdown

#### Session 1: "Who Am I Talking To?" — Stranger Danger Online (30 min)

**Objective:** Identify familiar vs. unfamiliar people online

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Show two cartoon characters: one waving from a playground, one peeking through a window. Ask: "How do you know which is safe?" |
| 5-12 min | Story Time | Read "The Internet Playground" (custom story about a child who meets three characters online — one friend, one stranger, one mystery). Pause for predictions. |
| 12-20 min | Group Discussion | Show screenshots of game avatars and video call windows. Students raise green/red cards to identify safe/unfamiliar contacts. |
| 20-27 min | Activity: "Name Tag Game" | Students create paper name tags with their online usernames. Practice asking: "Do I know this person in real life?" before chatting. |
| 27-30 min | Wrap-Up | Sing the "Safe Stranger Song" (to tune of "If You're Happy and You Know It"): *"If you know them, give a thumbs up! If you don't, wave hello!"* |

**Materials:** Story handout, green/red cards, paper name tags, markers

---

#### Session 2: "The Password Lock" — Keys to Your Door (30 min)

**Objective:** Understand passwords as keys and practice keeping them secret

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Bring a real padlock and key. Show how it locks a box. Ask: "What locks your tablet or game?" |
| 5-12 min | Direct Instruction | Explain that passwords are like digital keys. Demonstrate: show a locked game account vs. an unlocked one (with screenshots). |
| 12-20 min | Craft Activity: "Build Your Lock" | Students create a paper padlock craft. On the front, they write their first password (e.g., favorite animal + number). Inside, they draw what's "locked" inside (their games, photos, drawings). |
| 20-27 min | Practice: "Say It Out Loud" | In pairs, students practice saying their passwords out loud (like secret codes) without sharing them. Emphasize: "Tell your parents, but not your friends." |
| 27-30 min | Wrap-Up | Quick quiz: Teacher says scenarios ("A friend asks your password"), students respond with thumbs up/down. |

**Materials:** Paper padlock templates, scissors, glue, markers, sample screenshots of locked/unlocked accounts

---

#### Session 3: "Clicking Safely" — Green vs. Red Buttons (30 min)

**Objective:** Distinguish safe links from suspicious ones on screen

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Show a tablet with two buttons: a green "Play" button and a red "Free Robux!" button. Ask which they'd click. Discuss answers. |
| 5-12 min | Direct Instruction | Explain that some online buttons are traps. Show examples of real vs. fake buttons (using printed screenshots from common games/apps). |
| 12-20 min | Game: "Tap the Green One" | Project a mock game/app screen with multiple buttons. Students come up one at a time to tap the safe button. Repeat with increasing difficulty. |
| 20-27 min | Group Activity: "Button Sort" | Give each group a set of printed screenshots showing various buttons/links. Sort them into "Safe Green Basket" and "Watch Out Red Basket." |
| 27-30 min | Wrap-Up | Review the rule: "If it looks too good to be true, ask first!" |

**Materials:** Tablet/demo device, printed button screenshots, green/red paper baskets or boxes, sorting worksheets

---

#### Session 4: "Screen Time Boundaries" — Healthy Tech Habits (30 min)

**Objective:** Recognize healthy vs. unhealthy screen time and create family agreements

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Show a cartoon of a child playing games for 8 hours straight vs. one who plays for 1 hour then goes outside. Ask which looks happier. |
| 5-12 min | Discussion: "How Do You Feel?" | Students share how they feel after long screen sessions (tired eyes, sore back, grumpy) vs. balanced days. Teacher records responses on board. |
| 12-20 min | Activity: "My Screen Time Pie" | Students color a paper pie chart representing a healthy day: school, homework, play outside, family time, screen time. Emphasize balance. |
| 20-27 min | Family Connection: "Screen Time Contract" | Distribute a simple parent-child screen time agreement form. Students take it home to discuss with families and sign together. |
| 27-30 min | Wrap-Up | Sing a quick rhyme: *"Screens are fun, but don't forget — look up, play outside, give it your best!"* |

**Materials:** Pie chart templates, sample cartoons, parent-child screen time contract forms

---

#### Session 5: "Digital Footprints" — What Stays Forever? (30 min)

**Objective:** Understand that online actions leave permanent traces

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Go outside (or use a water tray indoors). Dip fingers in water and make footprints. Ask: "What happens when the sun comes out?" (They disappear.) Now show a photo of a footprint in mud — it stays longer. |
| 5-12 min | Story & Discussion | Explain that online actions are like footprints in digital mud — some wash away, but many stay forever. Share examples: a baby photo, a game screenshot, a comment. |
| 12-20 min | Activity: "Footprint Art" | Students create a paper "digital footprint" collage. On one side, they draw something that fades (a bubble). On the other, something permanent (a stone carving of their online name). |
| 20-27 min | Group Share | Volunteers share what permanent digital footprints they've already made (e.g., "I posted a drawing on Instagram"). Teacher validates and asks: "Would you be okay if your principal saw this?" |
| 27-30 min | Wrap-Up | Introduce the rule: "Think before you click — would you wear it to school?" |

**Materials:** Water tray or outdoor space, paper, markers, printed examples of permanent vs. temporary online content

---

#### Session 6: "Review & Celebration" — Digital Safety Superheroes (30 min)

**Objective:** Review all concepts and celebrate learning with certificates

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Play a quick review game: Teacher describes a scenario, students respond with hand signals (thumbs up for safe, thumbs down for unsafe). |
| 5-15 min | Station Rotation (3 stations × 3 min each) | **Station A:** Button sorting game<br>**Station B:** Password lock craft showcase<br>**Station C:** Digital footprint reflection drawing |
| 15-22 min | Group Discussion: "What Did We Learn?" | Students share one thing they're proud of knowing. Teacher reinforces key rules from Sessions 1-5. |
| 22-27 min | Certificate Ceremony | Each student receives a "Digital Safety Superhero" certificate with their name and a superhero title (e.g., "Captain Click-Safe," "The Password Protector"). |
| 27-30 min | Wrap-Up | Group photo with crafts and certificates. Send home with parent note summarizing the 5 key rules learned. |

**Materials:** Review game cards, station materials, certificates, superhero title stamps or stickers, camera for photos

---

### 📝 Assessment Methods

| Method | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Observational** | Teacher notes during activities: Can the student identify safe vs. unsafe links? Do they understand password secrecy? |
| **Oral Questioning** | Quick check-ins after each session: "What's a digital footprint?" "Why do we need passwords?" |
| **Art/Craft Assessment** | Review padlock crafts and footprint collages for understanding of core concepts |
| **Exit Tickets** | Simple draw-and-write: "Draw one thing you learned about staying safe online" |

---

### 🔑 Teacher Tips

- **Keep language simple:** Use "door lock" instead of "authentication," "tricky button" instead of "hyperlink."
- **Use repetition:** Revisit the same concepts across sessions with different activities.
- **Validate feelings:** Some students may feel anxious about online safety. Reassure them: "Mistakes are how we learn!"
- **Parent involvement is key:** Send home all contracts and certificates so families reinforce learning.
- **Adapt for special needs:** Provide larger print, audio stories, or hands-on manipulatives as needed.

### 📎 Appendix: Sample Story — "The Internet Playground"

*(Full story text for teacher use during Session 1)*

> Lily opened her tablet and went to the Internet Playground. First, she met Max, who played soccer with her every day at school. "Hey, want to play our game?" Max asked. ✅ Safe!
> 
> Then, a character in a purple hat waved from behind a tree. "I'm PurpleHat! I have free gold coins!" Lily wasn't sure if she knew him. ⚠️ Maybe suspicious!
> 
> Finally, Lily's teacher Mrs. Chen appeared on a video call. "Great job on your project, Lily!" 👍 Very safe!
> 
> Lily learned: Always ask — do I know this person in real life?
