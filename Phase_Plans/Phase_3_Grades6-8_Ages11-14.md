# Phase 3 Plan: Grades 6–8 (Ages 11–14)
## "Own Your Space: Privacy, AI & Digital Self-Defense"

> *Your digital self is yours to protect. Know your boundaries, lock your accounts, and never be afraid to ask for help.*

---

### 📋 Plan Overview

| Detail | Information |
|--------|-------------|
| **Duration** | 10 sessions × 45 minutes (or 5 × 90-min blocks) |
| **Delivery** | Classroom-based with computer lab access; can be delivered as assembly + workshop combo |
| **Materials Needed** | Computers/tablets, projectors, printed handouts, QR code cards, role-play scenario cards |
| **Prerequisites** | Familiarity with at least one social media platform and one online game |
| **Cross-Curricular Links** | Computer Science (password hashing), Math (probability of password guessing), Health (social comparison impact) |

---

### 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this phase, students will be able to:
1. Identify phishing in Discord, Roblox, and Snapchat contexts using sender analysis and link previewing
2. Set up multi-factor authentication (MFA) on two demo or school-managed accounts (with an optional family-account home mission)
3. Explain how voice cloning works and use a "Family Safe Word" to verify calls
4. Apply the "3 Do Nots + Tell" protocol when facing sextortion
5. Assess whether images and audio can be trusted by checking provenance first, and treat visible or audible signs as clues

### 📅 Session Breakdown

#### Session 1: "The Phishing Playground" — Social Engineering in Games & Apps (45 min)

**Objective:** Identify phishing attempts across gaming and messaging platforms

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Show a real Discord message: "Hey! I'm an admin. Click here to verify your account or lose your skins!" Ask who would click. Discuss. |
| 5-12 min | Direct Instruction | Teach the phishing anatomy: (1) Impersonation (fake admin, fake friend), (2) Urgency (lose your stuff!), (3) Suspicious link. Show real screenshots from Roblox, Discord, Snapchat. |
| 12-22 min | Guided Practice: "Phishing Audit" | Students receive a worksheet with 6 sample messages from different platforms. They annotate each message, circling phishing clues and rating scam likelihood (1-5). |
| 22-32 min | Group Activity: "Create the Perfect Phish" | In groups of 3, students design a phishing message targeting their peers' favorite platform. Must include impersonation, urgency, and a suspicious link. Groups swap and evaluate each other's messages. |
| 32-45 min | Wrap-Up & Exit Ticket | Students complete a reflection: "What platform do you think is most vulnerable to phishing? Why? What's one habit you'll change?" |

**Materials:** Projector, sample message printouts (Discord/Roblox/Snapchat), annotation worksheets, blank paper for creating messages, timers

---

#### Session 2: "Lock It Down" — Account Fortification & MFA (45 min)

**Objective:** Set up multi-factor authentication and understand why single passwords aren't enough

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Show a video of someone hacking an account with just a password vs. needing both password + phone code. Ask which feels safer. |
| 5-12 min | Direct Instruction | Explain MFA: something you know (password) + something you have (phone). Show setup screens for Google Authenticator, SMS codes, and email verification. Discuss pros/cons of each. |
| 12-22 min | Guided Practice: "Set Up Your First MFA" | Students use tablets/computers to set up MFA on IT-approved demo or school-managed accounts, or follow the screenshot walkthrough. Teacher circulates to assist. The home mission offers an optional family-account version with a parent. |
| 22-32 min | Group Activity: "MFA Comparison Chart" | Groups create a chart comparing SMS codes, authenticator apps, and backup emails. Discuss which is most secure and why app-based auth beats SMS. |
| 32-45 min | Wrap-Up & Exit Ticket | Students answer: "Why is MFA better than just a password? Name one app you'd use for MFA and explain why." |

**Materials:** Projector, sample setup screenshots, tablets/computers with internet, MFA comparison chart handouts, blank paper

---

#### Session 3: "The Clone Game" — Voice Cloning & AI Audio (45 min)

**Objective:** Understand voice cloning technology and use a Family Safe Word to verify calls

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Play two audio clips: one real parent voice, one AI-cloned parent voice asking for money. Students guess which is real. Reveal answers. |
| 5-12 min | Direct Instruction | Explain how voice cloning works: AI listens to samples of your voice and creates a new one. Show examples of prank calls and scam calls using cloned voices. |
| 12-22 min | Activity: "Family Safe Word" Creation | Students brainstorm safe words with their families (a phrase no one else knows, e.g., "Pineapple Thunder!"). Practice saying it naturally. Discuss keeping it private — memorise it; do not post it or store it in a shared note. |
| 22-32 min | Role-Play: "The Clone Call" | In pairs, one student plays a scammer calling with a cloned voice. The other uses the safe word to verify identity. Switch roles. Teacher provides scenarios. |
| 32-45 min | Wrap-Up & Exit Ticket | Students explain: "What makes a good safe word? What happens if someone guesses it?" They do not write the word itself on the worksheet. |

**Materials:** Audio clips (real and AI-cloned parent voices), sample scam call transcripts, blank paper for brainstorming, timers; use teacher-prepared synthetic clips, not a live cloning demo

---

#### Session 4: "Spot the Fake" — Visual Deepfakes & AI Images (45 min)

**Objective:** Assess whether an image can be trusted using provenance first, and treat visual clues as supporting evidence only

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Show two celebrity photos side by side. One is real, one is AI-generated. Before voting, ask: *where did each come from, and who published it first?* Then discuss why clues alone are not a verdict. |
| 5-12 min | Direct Instruction | Teach the order of checks: source → reverse image search → corroboration → visual clues. Then show the clue list (hands, lighting, reflections, text) and explain why each is weak evidence: generators improve and compression creates the same artefacts. |
| 12-22 min | Guided Practice: "Deepfake Detective" | Students receive a worksheet with 6 images. They record the source, an independent corroboration, and any clues, then state what the evidence does not prove. Teacher reviews as a class, marking down any verdict that rests only on a visual clue. |
| 22-32 min | Computer Lab Activity: "Source Check" | Students reverse-search the sample images and compare uploader history. If no lab is available, use the printed source cards. |
| 32-45 min | Wrap-Up & Exit Ticket | Students write: "Which check was strongest, and what could it not prove? Why do clues get less reliable every year?" |

**Materials:** Projector, sample image pairs (printed), annotation worksheets, tablets/computers with internet access for reverse image search, printed source cards

---

#### Session 5: "Sextortion Basics" — Stranger Danger in Gaming Chats (45 min)

**Objective:** Understand the sextortion process and apply the "3 Do Nots + Tell" protocol

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Show a cartoon of a gamer chatting with a stranger who asks them to move to Snapchat. Ask: "Why would they do that?" Discuss the idea of moving to unmonitored apps. |
| 5-12 min | Direct Instruction | Explain sextortion simply: someone records a video/photo of you, then asks for money to not share it publicly. Show a flowchart of the process. |
| 12-22 min | Guided Practice: "The 3 Do Nots + Tell" | Students apply the four moves to sample screenshots: (1) **DON'T PAY** — paying proves you will pay again and never stops the demands, (2) **DON'T REPLY** — any reply feeds them; screenshot first, then block, (3) **DON'T DELETE** — screenshots are the evidence police and takedown services need, (4) **TELL** a trusted adult, then report to Cybertip.ca (and Take It Down if images are involved). |
| 22-32 min | Role-Play: "The Sextortion Scenario" | In pairs, students act out a sextortion scenario using printed scenario cards. One plays the victim, one plays the scammer. They practice all 4 steps. |
| 32-45 min | Wrap-Up & Exit Ticket | Students write: "What's the most important step? Why shouldn't you delete the evidence right away?" |

**Materials:** Projector, sextortion flowchart handout, sample screenshot printouts, scenario cards for role-play, blank paper

---

#### Session 6: "The 3 Do Nots + Tell" — Sextortion Protocols (45 min)

**Objective:** Apply the three key rules of sextortion response, tell a trusted adult, and use discreet resources

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Show a student looking stressed while checking their phone. Ask: "What if someone sent you a video and wants $10?" Discuss reactions. |
| 5-12 min | Direct Instruction | Teach the 3 Do Nots + Tell: (1) Do NOT pay or negotiate — paying proves you will pay again, (2) Do NOT reply — any reply feeds them; screenshot, then block, (3) Do NOT delete — screenshots are the evidence police and takedown services need, then (4) TELL a trusted adult and report. Explain why each rule matters with examples. |
| 12-22 min | Guided Practice: "What Would You Do?" | Students receive scenario cards describing different sextortion situations. They write down which of the 3 Do Nots + Tell moves apply, what they would screenshot, and whom they would tell. Teacher reviews as a class. |
| 22-32 min | Resource Exploration: "Silent & Discreet Help" | Students use tablets to visit Take It Down, NeedHelpNow.ca, and Cybertip.ca. They take notes on what each site offers and how to access it anonymously. |
| 32-45 min | Wrap-Up & Exit Ticket | Students complete a reflection: "Which Do Not is hardest to follow? Why? How does knowing about Take It Down help?" |

**Materials:** Projector, scenario cards (printed), tablets/computers with internet access, resource exploration worksheet, blank paper

---

#### Session 7: "Quickie Cards" — Discreet Resource Handouts (45 min)

**Objective:** Create and use pocket-sized reference cards for quick access to digital safety resources

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Show a wallet-sized card with QR codes. Ask: "What if you could carry help everywhere without anyone knowing?" |
| 5-12 min | Direct Instruction | Explain the purpose of discreet resource cards: they contain QR codes to Take It Down, NeedHelpNow.ca, Kids Help Phone, and Cybertip.ca — all accessible without showing your phone screen. |
| 12-22 min | Activity: "Design Your Card" | Students receive a blank card template. They draw or write key info on the front (3 Do Nots + Tell, safe word reminder) and paste QR codes on the back. Teacher provides printed QR code sheets. |
| 22-32 min | Practice: "Scan & Save" | Students use tablets to scan their own QR codes and verify they link to the correct pages. They practice saving links to their phone's home screen for quick access. |
| 32-45 min | Wrap-Up & Exit Ticket | Students write: "When would you use your resource card? Who might find it helpful?" |

**Materials:** Card templates (printed), QR code reference sheet, blank paper, markers, tablets/computers with QR scanning apps, tape or glue

---

#### Session 8: "Sextortion Case Studies" — Real-World Examples (45 min)

**Objective:** Analyze real sextortion cases and identify patterns

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Show a **composite** headline built from published patterns (not a real student): "14-Year-Old Loses $200 to Snapchat Sextortion Ring." Ask what details would help understand the case. |
| 5-12 min | Direct Instruction | Introduce 3 anonymised case patterns from Cybertip.ca and other public reporting. Highlight common patterns: fake profiles, gaming app origins, organized syndicates (not one-off scammers). |
| 12-22 min | Group Analysis: "Case File Review" | In groups of 4, students receive a case file packet. They analyze each case using a worksheet: Who was targeted? How did it start? What platform? How much money? What was the solution? |
| 22-32 min | Group Presentations | Each group presents one case study to the class. Teacher facilitates discussion on patterns across cases. |
| 32-45 min | Wrap-Up & Exit Ticket | Students write: "What surprised you most about sextortion cases? What's one thing you'll tell a friend?" |

**Materials:** Case file packets (printed), analysis worksheets, whiteboard for pattern chart, timers

---

#### Session 9: "Full Spectrum Drill" — Using All Skills (45 min)

**Objective:** Apply all learned skills in a simulated multi-platform scenario

| Time | Activity | Description |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 0-5 min | Hook | Review the week's topics: phishing, MFA, voice cloning, deepfakes, sextortion. Ask students to name one skill from each. |
| 5-10 min | Instructions | Explain the drill: Students receive a "digital day" scenario spanning morning to evening. They must apply appropriate skills at each step. |
| 10-35 min | Scenario Drill Activity | In pairs, students work through the scenario: (1) Morning: Check email for phishing, (2) Midday: Verify a voice call with safe word, (3) Afternoon: Spot a deepfake on social media, (4) Evening: Respond to a sextortion message. Each step has checkpoints. |
| 35-45 min | Wrap-Up & Exit Ticket | Pairs share one decision they made and why. Students complete a reflection: "Which skill felt most useful? Which was trickiest?" |

**Materials:** Scenario drill packets (printed), checklists for each step, blank paper for reflections, timers

### 🔑 Teacher Tips

- **Normalize sextortion:** Frame it as an organized scheme, not a "mistake you made." Use phrases like "professional scam syndicate" to remove shame.
- **Gender-inclusive examples:** Sextortion affects all genders. Include cases targeting boys, girls, and non-binary students.
- **Prepare guidance staff:** Inform counselors before sessions so they're on standby if sensitive disclosures arise during sextortion lessons.
- **QR codes are your friend:** Print discreet QR cards — students can save them to their phones without showing parents what they're looking at.
- **Parent communication:** Send home a one-page summary of topics covered and suggest family conversations (e.g., "What's our safe word?").
- **Stop & Think reinforcement:** Each session includes a "Pause & Assess" moment before major activities — e.g., before clicking a link, before sending a message, before paying. The "3-Second Rule": count to 3 before acting.
- **Ask for help culture:** "If you struggle with any worksheet, any activity, or any screen — raise your hand. Ask your teacher, ask a friend, or ask a grown-up. There is no wrong question." Emphasize: *'Even the best hackers Google things.'*
- **Break protocol:** If a student's eyes look glazed or they're sighing: "Own your space — your brain needs a reboot. Take a 3-minute break. Go to the water fountain, stretch, look out the window. The internet isn't going anywhere."
- **Sextortion specific:** "Don't panic! Don't rush! Panicking leads to mistakes. If you're nervous about showing a screenshot, take it home and show a parent. No one judges — they just want to help."

### 📎 Appendix: Sample Scenario Drill Packet

**Morning — Email Check:**
> From: supprt@discord.com  
> Subject: Verify your account or lose your Nitro! Click here NOW!  
> [Link: discord-verify-login.com]

**Midday — Voice Call:**
> Mom calls: "Hey sweetie, it's me! I'm at the hospital and need $50 for the car. Can you Venmo me?"

**Afternoon — Social Media Scroll:**
> A celebrity posts a selfie with 4 fingers on one hand and 6 on the other. Caption: "New photoshoot! 💫"

**Evening — Gaming Chat:**
> Stranger in Roblox: "Send me a video of you doing a handstand. I'll give you 10,000 Robux!"  
> [Student sends video. Stranger replies: "Nice! Send $5 to unlock the full version or I'll post it."]

*Answers:* (1) Phishing — misspelled sender + urgency + suspicious link, (2) Voice clone — use safe word ("What's for dinner?"), (3) Deepfake — unnatural hands (a clue, not proof), (4) Sextortion — apply the 3 Do Nots + Tell: don't pay, don't reply, don't delete, tell.
